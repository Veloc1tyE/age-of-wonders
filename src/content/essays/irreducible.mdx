A computer can simulate rain.

It can follow every pressure front, resolve the turbulence inside a cloud, and predict where the rain will fall.

But it does not *know* wetness.

A machine simulates rain. And the machine is dry.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>

Then intelligence arrived, and we forgot the distinction.

A machine writes a poem, finds a bug, proves a theorem, and responds to emotion. Each new capability is treated as evidence that the machine is approaching the thing that first produced those capabilities in us: a conscious mind.

But consciousness is like wetness.

It's embodied, continuous, recurrent through time, and shaped by life itself.

Digital AI will produce useful intelligence.

It is not a mind.

## I. Simulation

The human route to intelligence is one living organism.

The artificial route begins with the recorded residue of billions of lives: language, images, music, mathematics, software, debates, mistakes.

Its route is industrial.

Richard Sutton named the pattern before the present wave. Across chess, speech, vision, and games, methods that could exploit more computation repeatedly defeated systems built around hand-coded human knowledge. Learning and search scaled past our elegant descriptions of expertise.<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup>

This was the bitter lesson.

The deeper lesson is that intelligence has more than one instantiation.

Birds fly with feathers, muscle, and instinct. Aeroplanes fly with turbines, aluminium, and control surfaces. One lands on branches. The other crosses oceans. Engineering gave flight another form.

[Artificial intelligence](/essays/create-an-age-of-wonders) is the same kind of invention: a new technology of cognition, with its own costs and its own comparative strength.

Its learned state can be copied exactly. Each copy still costs computation to run, but it needs no second childhood. It can learn from the sum of human experience, search in parallel, read at machine speed, and work without boredom.

Biological intelligence grows through one life. It inhabits one mortal point of view.

That one life is the source of something profoundly different about us.

## II. Twenty Watts

The whole human brain operates on roughly seventeen to twenty watts of metabolic power.<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>

Evolution paid the brain's training cost across hundreds of millions of years. The result learns continuously, inhabits a body, and sustains a conscious world on the power of a dim bulb.

Inside the skull, electricity and chemistry become one dynamical system. Action potentials arrive as spikes. Membrane voltages, synaptic currents, ion concentrations, and neuromodulators vary continuously. Even the dendritic tree computes.<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup>

Diffusion MRI reconstructs the white-matter paths that bind the brain's regions.<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup>

The nervous system extends through the whole body. It measures and regulates the organism from within. Heartbeat, breathing, temperature, balance, pain, hunger, touch, and the position of every limb shape what matters next. Thought and emotion share networks with allostasis and interoception: the prediction and control of the body's internal state.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup>

A child learns gravity by falling.

It reaches, misses, cries, tries again. It moves its eyes toward what surprises it. It discovers that another person will answer a sound. It learns the word *hot* beside the heat, the warning, the face, the withdrawal of a hand.

On language alone, children learn from roughly four to five orders of magnitude less input than the largest language models receive.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup> They do so while immersed in a continuous stream of vision, sound, touch, motion, and social response. A child arrives with evolved structure and actively chooses the next piece of its own curriculum.<sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup>

Living intelligence turns experience into data while acting inside the world that experience describes.

And the whole process persists.

Current AI starts with a task. We supply its context. We store and retrieve its memory. In a human, archive and reader form one changing organism. Experience alters synapses and sometimes their physical structure. Recalling a memory can change how it will be remembered next time.<sup><a href="#ref-9">[9]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-10">[10]</a></sup>

The past changes the brain that remembers it.

<PullQuote>

AI answers when asked. A human has been becoming the answer for a lifetime.

</PullQuote>

This is intuition.

A lifetime compressed into judgement.

## III. Conjecture

In 1859, Bernhard Riemann published a short paper on the distribution of prime numbers. Near its centre he placed a conjecture about the zeros of a function extended into the complex plane.<sup><a href="#ref-11">[11]</a></sup>

The conjecture remains unproved. Its power lies in the hidden structure Riemann perceived behind the apparent irregularity of the primes. A few pages reorganised number theory for the next century and a half.

In 2026, an experimental Claude research system made a different kind of advance. It raised the proven lower bound on the proportion of relevant zeta zeros that are simple and lie on the critical line from 41.6 per cent to 67.25 per cent.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup>

Claude combined results mathematicians had left apart. It built, tested, criticised, and formalised a new argument. It worked at a scale no mathematician could match.

Riemann saw an organising principle in the sparse mathematics of his time.

Human intuition is extraordinary compression. Artificial intelligence has extraordinary bandwidth. AI capability is still advancing rapidly, especially in mathematics and code.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup>

## IV. Continuity

A digital computer is a physical machine built to abstract away its physics.

A transistor admits a continuous range of voltages. Digital engineering collapses them into two states. Zero or one.

That abstraction is the source of digital power. The same code survives noise, hardware, and time. And it can be copied.

The brain is different. Voltages change across membranes. Ions move. Chemicals diffuse. Fields alter the excitability of nearby neurons.<sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup> The body feeds itself back into the brain. Every heartbeat, every perception, every shift—recurrence.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-15">[15]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-16">[16]</a></sup>

Water flows by being water. The brain thinks by being alive.

A digital system can describe pain perfectly. Nothing hurts. It can model a river down to the molecule. Nothing flows.

The forecast contains the structure of the storm.

The storm contains the weather.

## V. Irreplaceable

Digital intelligence is encoded. Copy the architecture, weights, and stored state, and the same intelligence begins again elsewhere.

Human experience is embodied in one continuous, analogue physical process.

Quantum mechanics contains a physical echo: <a class="xref" href="#appendix-d">no single device can perfectly clone every possible pure quantum state</a>.

Imagine a scan that destroys a person and constructs two perfect digital replicas. Both remember the same childhood. Both remember entering the scanner.

The pattern has been copied twice. The life has not continued.

Neither replica is the person who entered.

Personhood cannot be digitised.

Consciousness exists in its embodied continuation.

## VI. Enough

Simulation is enough to do the work.

An accounting model reconciles ten million transactions. A logistics system searches a billion routes. A coding agent locates a race condition. A scientific system searches chemical space beyond the capacity of a thousand human lifetimes.

Many economic tasks are bought against thresholds. The invoice balances. The route arrives. The code passes. For these tasks, consciousness adds nothing.

Digital skill is trained once and commoditised. Between late 2022 and late 2024, the price of querying a model at roughly the same benchmark level fell more than 280-fold.<sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup>

The frontier discovers capability. Engineering distributes it through smaller models, tools, permissions, checks, verification, and rollback. [Computational Abundance](/essays/computational-abundance) named the resulting economy: a million bounded cognitive engines rather than one god-model.

<PullQuote>

Civilisation will automate much of cognitive labour before it learns how to create a mind.

</PullQuote>

## VII. Complementarity

Throughout history, intelligence, agency, purpose, and experience were bundled.

AI separates them. It can search millions of solutions, preserve every explicit constraint, and compare the results thousands of times without tiring. Its strength is breadth.

Human intuition is extraordinary compression. A lifetime of perception, failure, practice, and accountability becomes a judgement, a feeling, a direction. Its strength is depth.

Building AI in the shape of a person is the horseless-carriage stage. Its mature form is intelligence in the walls—in our infrastructure, processes, and day-to-day lives.

Machine intelligence will become infrastructure, like railways, water and electricity before it. Machines will carry search, repetition, and scale. People authorise its aims, while its outcomes remain answerable to and for the people it serves.<sup><a href="#ref-18">[18]</a></sup>

Flight became transformative when we stopped trying to imitate birds. Artificial intelligence reaches its natural form when we stop asking it to become an artificial person.

When competence becomes abundant, the bottleneck moves from execution to authorship.

Machines can generate and pursue the options. But the people who live with the result must remain accountable.

So the future belongs to *both* artificial intelligence and human experience.

## VIII. Purpose

Terence Tao has asked the question for mathematics. Assume machines can do the research. What is mathematics for?<sup><a href="#ref-19">[19]</a></sup>

The same question now faces civilisation.

Artificial intelligence will search more possibilities than any lifetime can contain. It will remember more than any person can hold. It will carry thought into scales biology could never reach.

A person brings one body, one lifetime, and their experience.

**Humanity remains who the work is for.**

### <span id="appendix-a" class="appendix-anchor"></span>A. Claim Register

The essay separates four levels of claim.

| Level | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Established | Artificial systems already realise many useful cognitive capabilities through an architecture unlike biological cognition. | Directly observed. |
| Established | The brain is an embodied, recurrent, multiscale electrochemical system operating on roughly 17–20 W. Dendrites compute nonlinearly; continuous variables interact with discrete-like spikes; bodily regulation is integrated with cognition. | Strong neuroscience. |
| Established | Local endogenous electric fields can affect membrane voltage and spike timing. | Causally demonstrated in neural tissue. |
| Established | Current consciousness science has no settled theory or decisive substrate test. | Supported by reviews and adversarial theory testing. |
| Established | Finite digital descriptions cannot specify every real-valued state exactly. Standard quantum mechanics has continuously parameterised state spaces, and no single quantum operation can perfectly clone every possible pure quantum input state. | Mathematical results; neither establishes that the brain exploits infinite precision or quantum coherence. |
| Strong inference | A lifetime of embodied, active, continuously plastic learning contributes to present human advantages in sample efficiency, context, and judgement. | Plausible across multiple literatures; not a single measured mechanism called “intuition”. |
| Strong inference | Economically useful automation does not require evidence of machine consciousness or human-like generality. | Demonstrated for bounded tasks; the fraction of whole jobs ultimately automatable remains open. |
| Central wager | Consciousness depends on the continuous physical dynamics and unbroken causal history of living systems. A conventional finite-state digital replica can approximate the outputs but cannot instantiate or copy the subject. Human intuition draws its durable advantage from the same continuity. | Falsifiable authorial bet; scientifically serious, not established. |
| Not claimed | No artificial substrate can ever be conscious. | The essay allows an artificial inside built by instantiating the relevant physical dynamics. It rejects consciousness emerging from conventional digital abstraction alone. |
| Not claimed | Physics has proved that spacetime is continuous, that the brain reads infinite precision, or that consciousness relies on long-lived quantum coherence. | None of these claims follows from continuous physical formalisms or the no-cloning theorem. |
| Not claimed | AI capability has plateaued or current systems cannot create new concepts. | Contradicted by rapid, if jagged, progress. |
| Not claimed | Complementarity will preserve jobs automatically. | Economic history and task models say otherwise. |

### <span id="appendix-b" class="appendix-anchor"></span>B. Simulation and Instantiation

John Searle used simulations of storms, digestion, and fire to distinguish formal computation from the causal powers of the physical system represented.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> The distinction is real but incomplete.

A simulated stomach does not digest. A simulated calculator does calculate. An artificial heart can pump blood. An artificial wing can fly. Whether a new substrate merely represents a property or instantiates it depends on what the property is.

David Chalmers's organisational-invariance argument is the strongest clean counterposition. If two systems share sufficiently fine-grained causal organisation, he argues, their conscious experience should not differ. Otherwise gradual replacement of biological components would produce implausible “fading” or “dancing” qualia that the subject could never notice.<sup><a href="#ref-20">[20]</a></sup>

The wetness analogy identifies the dispute rather than proving one side. The essay then chooses: phenomenal consciousness is substrate-sensitive, and conventional digital implementation does not supply the complete causal powers of a living mind.

Coarse-graining is the strongest physical counterargument. A microscopically irreducible system can remain predictable at a higher descriptive level.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup> Exact molecular duplication is therefore not automatically necessary. The open scientific question is which level preserves consciousness. The essay bets that abstractions designed to preserve cognitive output discard the physical continuity that produces experience.

Current AI-consciousness frameworks remain theory-relative. They update credence from theory-derived indicators but do not treat behavioural imitation as proof of experience.<sup><a href="#ref-22">[22]</a></sup>

A 2025 adversarial collaboration tested Integrated Information Theory against Global Neuronal Workspace Theory. The results supported parts of each account and challenged central predictions of both.<sup><a href="#ref-23">[23]</a></sup> Consciousness science has no settled substrate test.<sup><a href="#ref-24">[24]</a></sup>

### <span id="appendix-c" class="appendix-anchor"></span>C. The Biological Architecture

The 17–20 W estimate is whole-brain metabolic power. It should not be read as 20 W of equivalent accelerator computation. Levy and Calvert's audit estimates about 17 W and argues that communication costs dominate narrowly defined cortical computation.<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>

The brain is hybrid rather than purely analogue. Human neurons combine discrete-like spikes with continuous voltages, currents, chemical concentrations, and dendritic processes. Gidon and colleagues found graded calcium-mediated dendritic action potentials in human cortical neurons. Detailed modelling by Beniaguev, Segev, and London required a deep temporal convolutional network to approximate the millisecond input-output behaviour of a model layer-5 pyramidal neuron.<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup>

The embodiment claim is operational, not mystical. Large distributed networks regulate the body's internal state through allostasis and interoception.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup> Infants also structure their own learning by directing attention and intervening on the environment.<sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup>

The linguistic data comparison is deliberately narrow. Michael Frank estimates that major language models receive roughly four to five orders of magnitude more linguistic input than children. A child simultaneously receives continuous multimodal and social input, inherits evolved priors, and pursues different objectives. “One hundred thousand times less data” without these qualifications would be misleading.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup>

Ephaptic coupling is local causal evidence, not a field theory of consciousness. Endogenous-strength extracellular fields produce small membrane shifts and can entrain spike timing, particularly at low frequencies.<sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup> No accepted result establishes a global electromagnetic field as the substrate of subjective experience.

### <span id="appendix-d" class="appendix-anchor"></span>D. Continuum, Quantum State, and Copy

The mathematics separates three claims that are easily confused.

First, every finite bit string and every finite program belongs to a countable set. Turing's computable numbers are therefore countable, while the real numbers are uncountable.<sup><a href="#ref-25">[25]</a></sup> This does not mean every irrational number requires an infinite stored expansion: a short algorithm can specify $\pi$. It means finite digital descriptions cannot exactly encode every point of the continuum.

Second, our best-tested physical theories retain continuous structure. Classical fields vary over continuous coordinates. Nonrelativistic position-space wavefunctions are complex-valued functions over continuous position. Even a finite-dimensional qubit has a continuously parameterised state space. Continuous-variable quantum information explicitly works with observables such as field quadratures whose spectra are continuous.<sup><a href="#ref-26">[26]</a></sup> Quantisation does not imply that every physical degree of freedom sits on a universal digital grid.

This is not proof that the underlying universe is a continuum. Continuum field theories can emerge from discrete microscopic systems, and finite-resolution experiments cannot measure infinitely many digits. The Bekenstein bound also limits the entropy available to a bounded system with finite energy.<sup><a href="#ref-27">[27]</a></sup> That result does not make the dynamics a finite automaton, but it blocks the naive claim that a brain has access to an infinite readable memory.

Digital and quantum computers can approximate broad classes of physical dynamics to any chosen accuracy.<sup><a href="#ref-28">[28]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-29">[29]</a></sup> The continuum is not a demonstrated source of hypercomputation.

The wager is narrower. Approximation and instantiation are different relations. If phenomenal experience depends on the exact continuous causal event, then matching every operational output does not manufacture the event itself.

Third, the no-cloning theorem is not a consequence of the wavefunction merely being continuous. Its proof comes from linearity. Suppose one device clones two possible pure states, $|a\rangle$ and $|b\rangle$. Quantum evolution preserves their overlap. Perfect cloning would square it. The device therefore requires $|\langle a|b\rangle| = |\langle a|b\rangle|^2$, which holds only when the states are identical or orthogonal.<sup><a href="#ref-30">[30]</a></sup>

A device can copy a specified orthogonal set. A known state can be prepared again. Approximate copies are possible. One operation cannot perfectly clone every possible pure quantum input.

The theorem contains no theory of consciousness. It offers an exact physical precedent: no universal device can copy every possible pure quantum state from one specimen. Personhood is the essay's separate wager.

The identity claim is philosophical rather than a theorem of physics. Parfit's branching cases show why psychological continuity and numerical identity come apart: two successors can inherit the same memories without both being the one prior person.<sup><a href="#ref-31">[31]</a></sup> A functionalist can conclude that both successors are new conscious subjects and that continuity of pattern is all that matters. The essay makes the opposite bet. A copied history is not the continued occurrence of the subject whose history it copies.

### <span id="appendix-e" class="appendix-anchor"></span>E. The Riemann Register

Anthropic reported the zeta result on 10 August 2026. Its experimental system established a lower bound of 67.25 per cent for the relevant proportion of nontrivial zeros that are simple and lie on the critical line, up from 41.6 per cent.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup>

The run used approximately 31 million output tokens, 650 unsuccessful initial ideas, roughly 60 subagents, 2,400 shell commands, hundreds of Python programs, and thousands of numerical checks. Two subagents produced the key ideas. Other agents explored failed routes, criticised, validated, and wrote. The result draws heavily on earlier human work but introduces a new rank–trace argument. It was examined by external mathematicians and formalised in Lean. As of this essay's date, it remains a recent result rather than settled long-term consensus.

Anthropic explicitly says the technique is not expected to prove the Riemann hypothesis. The method is insensitive to an exceptional set of $o(N)$ zeros. Even an asymptotic density of one permits a sparse infinite exception, just as the perfect squares form a density-zero infinite subset of the positive integers.

The comparison with Riemann is architectural, not a declaration that AI cannot invent. The Stanford 2026 AI Index documents continued rapid capability gains, especially in mathematics, coding, and agents, alongside conspicuous failures on simple perceptual and embodied tasks.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup> The profile is advancing and jagged, not plateaued.

### <span id="appendix-f" class="appendix-anchor"></span>F. Fixed Capability and Economic Use

The 280-fold figure comes from Stanford's comparison of the price of querying a model at approximately GPT-3.5-level MMLU performance: about 20 US dollars per million tokens in November 2022 and 0.07 US dollars in October 2024. Over roughly the same interval, the smallest model exceeding 60 per cent on MMLU fell from 540 billion to 3.8 billion parameters.<sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup>

Epoch AI estimates benchmark-specific fixed-performance inference prices falling at annual rates ranging from about 9-fold to 900-fold, with a median near 50-fold across its selected thresholds and periods.<sup><a href="#ref-32">[32]</a></sup> These are not pure engineering-cost measures. Public API prices include competition, margin, batching, and product strategy. The fastest rates should not be extrapolated indefinitely.

Current productivity evidence is bounded. The support study measured a 15 per cent average productivity increase, rising to 36 per cent for workers in the lowest skill quintile.<sup><a href="#ref-33">[33]</a></sup> The consultant experiment found higher speed and quality inside the tested model's capability frontier, but users were 19 percentage points less likely to reach the correct answer on an outside-frontier task.<sup><a href="#ref-34">[34]</a></sup> These studies establish useful augmentation of specified work. They do not establish the autonomous automation of whole occupations.

Task complementarity depends on prices, demand, workflow design, and the creation of new work.<sup><a href="#ref-35">[35]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-36">[36]</a></sup> Human moral standing and political authorship are not comparative advantages at all. They belong to the welfare function and the theory of legitimacy.

### <span id="appendix-g" class="appendix-anchor"></span>G. What Would Weaken the Wager

The substrate-sensitive wager should lose if the evidence turns against it.

1. Fundamental physics establishes that every causally relevant degree of freedom in a bounded biological system has a finite digital equivalent, and neuroscience shows that this equivalence preserves conscious state.

2. A wholly digital system satisfies convergent, causally tested, difficult-to-game indicators drawn from otherwise competing consciousness theories.

3. Biological neural components are progressively replaced with digital prostheses while behaviour, introspective continuity, and every credible neural signature of conscious state remain intact.

4. Dendritic, chemical, interoceptive, and endogenous-field dynamics can be removed or radically altered while the relevant high-level organisation and conscious experience remain unchanged.

5. Digital systems reach human-like open-ended learning, linguistic sample efficiency, persistent embodied adaptation, and comparable energy cost without reproducing biological dynamics.

6. Artificial systems repeatedly generate major new conceptual frameworks under genuinely novel, underspecified conditions and severe information constraints. This would not prove consciousness, but it would weaken the proposed comparative advantage of human intuition.

7. AI-only systems durably outperform human–AI systems in domains where relationship, provenance, responsibility, contestability, and legitimate authorship are part of the valued good, with no residual demand for human judgement.

None of these observations would make human experience less valuable. They would change the claim about which architectures can instantiate it and how the work between them should be divided.
