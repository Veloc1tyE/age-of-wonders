A computer can simulate rain.

It can follow every pressure front, resolve the turbulence inside a cloud, and predict where the rain will fall.

But it does not *know* wetness.

A machine simulates rain. And the machine is dry.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>

Then intelligence arrived, and we forgot the distinction.

A machine writes a poem, finds a bug, proves a theorem, and responds to emotion. We treat each new capability as evidence that the machine is approaching what produced those capabilities in us: a conscious mind.

But consciousness is like wetness.

It's embodied, continuous, recurrent through time, and shaped by life itself.

Digital AI will produce useful intelligence. 

And it is not a mind.

## I. Simulation

The human route to intelligence is one living organism.

The artificial route begins with the recorded residue of billions of lives: language, images, music, mathematics, software, debates, mistakes.

Its method is industrial.

Richard Sutton named the pattern before the present wave. Across chess, speech, vision, and games, methods that could exploit more computation repeatedly defeated systems built around hand-coded human knowledge. Learning and search scaled past our elegant descriptions of expertise.<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup>

That was the bitter lesson.

The deeper lesson is that intelligence has more than one form.

Birds fly with feathers, muscle, and instinct. Aeroplanes fly with turbines, aluminium, and control surfaces. One lands on branches. The other crosses oceans. Engineering gave flight another form.

[Artificial intelligence](/essays/create-an-age-of-wonders) is the same kind of invention: a new technology of cognition, with its own costs and its own comparative strength.

Its learned state can be copied exactly. Each copy still costs computation to run, but it needs no second childhood. It can learn from a vast record of human experience, search in parallel, read at machine speed, and work without boredom.

Biological intelligence emerges from one mortal life.

## II. Twenty Watts

The whole human brain operates on seventeen to twenty watts of metabolic power.<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>

Evolution paid the brain's training cost across hundreds of millions of years. The result learns continuously, inhabits a body, and sustains a conscious world on the power of a dim bulb.

Inside the skull, electricity and chemistry become one dynamical system. Action potentials arrive as spikes. Membrane voltages, synaptic currents, ion concentrations, and neuromodulators vary continuously. Even the dendritic tree computes.<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup>

Diffusion MRI reconstructs the white-matter paths that bind the brain's regions.<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup>

The nervous system extends through the whole body. It measures and regulates the organism from within. Heartbeat, breathing, temperature, balance, pain, hunger, touch, and the position of every limb shape what matters next. Thought and emotion share networks with allostasis and interoception: the prediction and control of the body's internal state.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup>

A child learns gravity by falling.

It reaches, misses, cries, tries again. It moves its eyes toward what surprises it. It discovers that another person will answer a sound. It learns the word *hot* beside the heat, the warning, the face, the withdrawal of a hand.

On language alone, children learn from roughly four to five orders of magnitude less input than the largest language models receive.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup> They do so while immersed in a continuous stream of vision, sound, touch, motion, and social response. A child arrives with evolved structure and actively chooses the next piece of its own curriculum.<sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup>

Living intelligence turns experience into data while acting inside the world that experience describes.

And the whole process persists.

Current AI starts with a task. We supply its context. We store and retrieve its memory. In a human, archive and reader form one changing organism. Experience alters synapses and sometimes their physical structure. Recalling a memory can change how it will be remembered next time.<sup><a href="#ref-9">[9]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-10">[10]</a></sup>

The past changes the brain that remembers it.

<PullQuote>

AI answers when asked. A human has been becoming the answer for a lifetime.

</PullQuote>

This is intuition.

A lifetime compressed into judgement.

## III. Conjecture

In 1859, Bernhard Riemann published a short paper on the distribution of prime numbers. Near its centre he placed a conjecture about the zeros of a function extended into the complex plane.<sup><a href="#ref-11">[11]</a></sup>

The conjecture remains unproved. Its power lies in the hidden structure Riemann perceived behind the apparent irregularity of the primes. A few pages reorganised number theory for the next century and a half.

Five years earlier, Riemann had lectured on the foundations of geometry. He described curved space decades before physics needed it. Sixty years later, his geometry became the mathematics of general relativity.<sup><a href="#ref-11">[11]</a></sup>

In 2026, an experimental Claude research system made a different kind of advance. It raised the proven lower bound on the proportion of relevant zeta zeros that are simple and lie on the critical line from 41.6 per cent to 67.25 per cent.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup>

Claude combined results mathematicians had left apart. It built, tested, criticised, and formalised a new argument. It worked at a scale no mathematician could match.

Riemann saw an organising principle in the sparse mathematics of his time.

Human intuition is extraordinary compression. Artificial intelligence has extraordinary bandwidth.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup>

## IV. Continuity

A digital computer is a physical machine built to abstract away its physics.

A transistor admits a continuous range of voltages. Digital engineering collapses them into two states. Zero or one.

That abstraction is the source of digital power. The same code survives noise, hardware, and time. And it can be copied.

The brain is different. Voltages change across membranes. Ions move. Chemicals diffuse. Fields alter the excitability of nearby neurons.<sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup> The body feeds itself back into the brain. Every heartbeat, every perception, every shift—recurrence.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-15">[15]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-16">[16]</a></sup>

The discarded physics is necessary for the world inside. Just as water flows by being water, the brain feels by being alive.

## V. Irreplaceable

Digital intelligence is encoded. Copy the architecture, weights, and stored state, and the same intelligence begins again elsewhere.

Human experience is embodied in one continuous physical process.

Imagine a scan that destroys a person and constructs two perfect digital replicas. Both remember the same childhood. Both remember entering the scanner.

The pattern has been copied twice. Neither continues the life that entered. 

Personhood cannot be transferred as information. 

Identity belongs to our trajectory through time.

## VI. Enough

Simulation is enough to do the work.

An accounting model reconciles ten million transactions. A logistics system searches a billion routes. A coding agent locates a race condition. A scientific system searches chemical space beyond the capacity of a thousand human lifetimes.

Many economic tasks are bought against thresholds. The invoice balances. The route arrives. The code passes. For these tasks, the output is enough.

A digital skill can be trained once, then copied. Between late 2022 and late 2024, the price of querying a model at roughly the same benchmark level fell more than 280-fold.<sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup>

The frontier discovers capability. Engineering distributes it through smaller models, tools, permissions, checks, verification, and rollback. [Computational Abundance](/essays/computational-abundance) named the resulting economy: a million bounded cognitive engines rather than one god-model.

<PullQuote>

Humanity will automate much of cognitive labour before it learns how to create a mind.

</PullQuote>

## VII. Complementarity

Throughout history, intelligence, agency, purpose, and experience were bundled.

AI separates them. It can search millions of solutions, preserve every explicit constraint, and compare the results thousands of times without tiring. Its strength is breadth.

Human intuition is extraordinary compression. A lifetime of perception, failure, practice, and accountability becomes a judgement, a feeling, a direction. Its strength is depth.

Building AI in the shape of a person is the horseless-carriage stage. Its mature form is intelligence in the walls: infrastructure, processes, daily life.

Machine intelligence will become like railways, water, and electricity before it. Machines will carry search, repetition, and scale. People authorise its aims. The systems remain answerable to those they serve.<sup><a href="#ref-18">[18]</a></sup>

Flight became transformative when we stopped trying to imitate birds. Artificial intelligence reaches its natural form when we stop asking it to become an artificial person.

Machines can generate and pursue the options. The people who live with the result must retain authority over the choice.

The future belongs to both artificial intelligence and human experience.

## VIII. Purpose

Terence Tao has asked the question for mathematics. Assume machines can do the research. What is mathematics for?<sup><a href="#ref-19">[19]</a></sup>

The same question now faces society.

Artificial intelligence will search more possibilities than any lifetime can contain. It will remember more than any person can hold. It will carry intelligence into scales biology could never reach.

A person brings one body, one lifetime, one point of view.

**Humanity remains who the work is for.**

### <span id="appendix-a" class="appendix-anchor"></span>A. Claim Register

*Four kinds of claim. Only two are bets.*

The essay separates four levels of claim.

| Level | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Established | Artificial systems already realise many useful cognitive capabilities through an architecture unlike biological cognition. | Directly observed. |
| Established | The brain is an embodied, recurrent, multiscale electrochemical system operating on roughly 17–20 W. Dendrites compute nonlinearly; continuous variables interact with discrete-like spikes; bodily regulation is integrated with cognition. | Strong neuroscience. |
| Established | Local endogenous electric fields can affect membrane voltage and spike timing. | Causally demonstrated in neural tissue. |
| Established | Current consciousness science has no settled theory or decisive substrate test. | Supported by reviews and adversarial theory testing. |
| Established | Finite digital descriptions cannot specify every real-valued state exactly. Standard quantum mechanics has continuously parameterised state spaces, and no single quantum operation can perfectly clone every possible pure quantum input state. | Mathematical results whose relevance to consciousness is the essay's central wager. |
| Strong inference | A lifetime of embodied, active, continuously plastic learning contributes to present human advantages in sample efficiency, context, and judgement. | Supported across multiple literatures. “Intuition” names their synthesis. |
| Strong inference | Economically useful automation does not require evidence of machine consciousness or human-like generality. | Demonstrated for bounded tasks. Whole-job automation depends on integrated task coverage. |
| Central wager: constitution | Consciousness is constituted by an embodied system's continuous physical dynamics. Digital abstraction preserves useful outputs by discarding dynamics constitutive of the subject. | The essay's position. Unproven in the strict sense. Evidenced by the substrate-sensitive biology of Appendix C and the abstraction argument of Appendix D. Appendix G names the evidence that would defeat it. |
| Central wager: persistence | Copying a complete informational state begins another causal history. The subject persists through the continued occurrence of its embodied process. | The essay's account of personal identity. Branching cases expose the choice. Holds whether or not the constitution wager holds. |
| Consequence | A conventional digital replica can reproduce intelligence without continuing the person or instantiating the same kind of subject. | The technological and civilisational conclusion. |

### <span id="appendix-b" class="appendix-anchor"></span>B. Simulation and Instantiation

*Whether a substrate represents a property or instantiates it depends on what the property is.*

John Searle used simulations of storms, digestion, and fire to distinguish formal computation from the causal powers of the physical system represented.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> That distinction begins the analysis.

A simulated stomach does not digest. A simulated calculator does calculate. An artificial heart can pump blood. An artificial wing can fly.

David Chalmers's organisational-invariance argument is the strongest clean counterposition. If two systems share sufficiently fine-grained causal organisation, he argues, their conscious experience should not differ. Otherwise gradual replacement of biological components would produce implausible “fading” or “dancing” qualia that the subject could never notice.<sup><a href="#ref-20">[20]</a></sup>

The wetness analogy locates the claim without deciding it. Which kind of property consciousness is remains the open question. The essay wagers that it is the stomach's kind: substrate-sensitive, and not supplied by conventional digital implementation. The evidence for that reading is the biology of Appendix C and the abstraction argument of Appendix D. The evidence against it is Chalmers's invariance argument.

Coarse-graining is the strongest physical counterargument. A microscopically irreducible system can remain predictable at a higher descriptive level.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup> Cognition can survive that reduction. The essay's position is that consciousness does not. Abstractions designed to preserve cognitive output discard the physical continuity that produces experience.

Physical detail can be dispensable to the output yet constitutive of the subject. That is the hinge. Output equivalence does not entail phenomenal equivalence.

Current AI-consciousness frameworks remain theory-relative. They update credence from theory-derived indicators and treat behavioural imitation as one signal among others.<sup><a href="#ref-22">[22]</a></sup>

A 2025 adversarial collaboration tested Integrated Information Theory against Global Neuronal Workspace Theory. The results supported parts of each account and challenged central predictions of both.<sup><a href="#ref-23">[23]</a></sup> Consciousness science has no settled substrate test.<sup><a href="#ref-24">[24]</a></sup>

### <span id="appendix-c" class="appendix-anchor"></span>C. The Biological Architecture

*Twenty watts, and the body is in the loop.*

The 17–20 W estimate is whole-brain metabolic power. Accelerator-equivalent computation is a different quantity. Levy and Calvert's audit estimates about 17 W and argues that communication costs dominate narrowly defined cortical computation.<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>

The brain is hybrid rather than purely analogue. Human neurons combine discrete-like spikes with continuous voltages, currents, chemical concentrations, and dendritic processes. Gidon and colleagues found graded calcium-mediated dendritic action potentials in human cortical neurons. Detailed modelling by Beniaguev, Segev, and London required a deep temporal convolutional network to approximate the millisecond input-output behaviour of a model layer-5 pyramidal neuron.<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup>

The embodiment claim is operational. Large distributed networks regulate the body's internal state through allostasis and interoception.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup> Infants also structure their own learning by directing attention and intervening on the environment.<sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup>

The linguistic data comparison is deliberately narrow. Michael Frank estimates that major language models receive roughly four to five orders of magnitude more linguistic input than children. A child simultaneously receives continuous multimodal and social input, inherits evolved priors, and pursues different objectives.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup>

Ephaptic coupling supplies local causal evidence. Endogenous-strength extracellular fields produce small membrane shifts and can entrain spike timing, particularly at low frequencies.<sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup> Its demonstrated scale is local.

### <span id="appendix-d" class="appendix-anchor"></span>D. Continuum, Chaos, Quantum State, and Copy

*A copy can shadow the orbit. It cannot be the orbit.*

The argument has two layers: a mathematical limit on exact digital continuation, and a physical claim about what consciousness is. The first is proved. The second is wagered.

First, every finite bit string and every finite program belongs to a countable set. Turing's computable numbers are therefore countable, while the real numbers are uncountable.<sup><a href="#ref-25">[25]</a></sup> A short algorithm can specify $\pi$, but finite digital descriptions cannot specify every point of the continuum.

Digital representation turns that limit into an engineering principle. It maps many lower-level physical states onto one logical state. Every voltage inside a noise margin becomes the same bit. This equivalence class gives software its portability, reliability, and perfect reproducibility.

Second, the brain leaves those physical variables active. Classical fields vary over continuous coordinates. Wavefunctions and even finite-dimensional quantum states are continuously parameterised. Continuous-variable quantum information works directly with observables whose spectra are continuous.<sup><a href="#ref-26">[26]</a></sup> The Bekenstein bound limits the distinguishable information in a bounded finite-energy system without turning its evolution into a finite automaton.<sup><a href="#ref-27">[27]</a></sup>

That bound is also the strongest objection to this layer. If continuum distinctions ever affect behaviour or report, they are measurable. What is measurable in a bounded finite-energy region is finitely describable. What is finitely describable can be digitally represented. The essay accepts each step. Its reply is the hinge of Appendix B: a representation of a distinction is not its occurrence. The continuum argument therefore does not prove the constitution wager on its own. It shows what digital abstraction discards, and leaves open whether the discarded motion is what experience is made of.

A digital computer is itself a continuous quantum object. Its logical architecture suppresses the causal relevance of most lower-level motion. A brain computes through membrane voltage, chemical concentration, field interaction, and bodily recurrence. The substrate thesis places consciousness in that active physical trajectory.

Digital and quantum computers can approximate broad classes of physical dynamics to any chosen accuracy.<sup><a href="#ref-28">[28]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-29">[29]</a></sup> That is why the function survives. Approximation relates a model to a process. Identity belongs to the continued process itself.

Continuous dynamics span both computable and noncomputable cases. Polynomial ordinary differential equations of polynomial length characterise polynomial-time computation.<sup><a href="#ref-30">[30]</a></sup> Specially constructed wave equations can also evolve computable initial data into a noncomputable solution, with the result turning on how the initial field is represented.<sup><a href="#ref-31">[31]</a></sup> The essay places consciousness in the causally active physical distinctions that conventional digital implementation throws away.

Third, long-horizon identity has an exact limit. A closed deterministic machine with $N$ possible states repeats within $N+1$ transitions and cycles thereafter. An aperiodic continuum trajectory does not. Where the dynamics have sensitive dependence, small differences in initial state grow until the trajectories separate macroscopically.<sup><a href="#ref-32">[32]</a></sup>

The theorem is precise:

> A fixed finite-state digital implementation cannot remain state-for-state equivalent for unbounded time to an aperiodic physical trajectory containing causally relevant continuum distinctions.

One constitutive continuum degree of freedom is enough to break exact instantiation. Sensitive dynamics make the difference compound through time. For computable dynamics, greater precision extends the chosen tracking horizon. The model still begins as a new physical process rather than the continued occurrence of the old one.

The theorem rules out exact continuation. It does not rule out organisational equivalence at a coarser grain, which is the functionalist's actual claim. That dispute returns to Appendix B.

Shadowing explains why the copy can remain functionally excellent. A numerical pseudo-orbit can follow a true orbit near the intended one for long periods.<sup><a href="#ref-33">[33]</a></sup> Coarse-grained predictions can survive microscopic irreducibility.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup> The output survives because it belongs to the equivalence class. The original subject does not, because it belongs to the trajectory.

Fourth, quantum mechanics supplies a physical precedent for limited copyability. Suppose one device clones two possible pure states, $|a\rangle$ and $|b\rangle$. Quantum evolution preserves their overlap. Perfect cloning would square it. The device therefore requires $|\langle a|b\rangle| = |\langle a|b\rangle|^2$, which holds only when the states are identical or orthogonal.<sup><a href="#ref-34">[34]</a></sup>

A device can copy a specified orthogonal set. A known state can be prepared again. Approximate copies are possible. One operation cannot perfectly clone every possible pure quantum input. Its role here is analogy: physical state is richer than universally clonable description.

Parfit's branching cases complete the identity argument. Two successors can inherit one psychological history. Numerical identity cannot branch.<sup><a href="#ref-35">[35]</a></sup> The essay locates survival in the unbroken causal process. A conscious copy would be a new subject. It would not be the continued life of the person copied.

Capability belongs to the pattern. Experience belongs to the process. Identity belongs to the trajectory.

### <span id="appendix-e" class="appendix-anchor"></span>E. The Riemann Register

*Two architectures of discovery. One saw. The other searched.*

Anthropic reported the zeta result on 10 August 2026. Its experimental system established a lower bound of 67.25 per cent for the relevant proportion of nontrivial zeros that are simple and lie on the critical line, up from 41.6 per cent.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup>

The run used approximately 31 million output tokens, 650 unsuccessful initial ideas, roughly 60 subagents, 2,400 shell commands, hundreds of Python programs, and thousands of numerical checks. Two subagents produced the key ideas. Other agents explored failed routes, criticised, validated, and wrote. The result draws heavily on earlier human work but introduces a new rank–trace argument. It was examined by external mathematicians and formalised in Lean. As of this essay's date, it remains a recent result rather than settled long-term consensus.

Anthropic explicitly says the technique is not expected to prove the Riemann hypothesis. The method is insensitive to an exceptional set of $o(N)$ zeros. Even an asymptotic density of one permits a sparse infinite exception, just as the perfect squares form a density-zero infinite subset of the positive integers.

The comparison with Riemann describes two architectures of discovery. The Stanford 2026 AI Index documents continued rapid capability gains, especially in mathematics, coding, and agents, alongside conspicuous failures on simple perceptual and embodied tasks.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup> The profile is advancing and jagged.

### <span id="appendix-f" class="appendix-anchor"></span>F. Fixed Capability and Economic Use

*A skill trained once is priced like a copy.*

The 280-fold figure comes from Stanford's comparison of the price of querying a model at approximately GPT-3.5-level MMLU performance: about 20 US dollars per million tokens in November 2022 and 0.07 US dollars in October 2024. Over roughly the same interval, the smallest model exceeding 60 per cent on MMLU fell from 540 billion to 3.8 billion parameters.<sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup>

Epoch AI estimates benchmark-specific fixed-performance inference prices falling at annual rates ranging from about 9-fold to 900-fold, with a median near 50-fold across its selected thresholds and periods.<sup><a href="#ref-36">[36]</a></sup> Public API prices combine engineering cost with competition, margin, batching, and product strategy. They describe a historical rate rather than a perpetual law.

Current productivity evidence is bounded. The support study measured a 15 per cent average productivity increase, rising to 36 per cent for workers in the lowest skill quintile.<sup><a href="#ref-37">[37]</a></sup> The consultant experiment found higher speed and quality inside the tested model's capability frontier, but users were 19 percentage points less likely to reach the correct answer on an outside-frontier task.<sup><a href="#ref-38">[38]</a></sup> These studies establish useful augmentation of specified work. Whole occupations require integrated coverage across the task frontier.

Task complementarity depends on prices, demand, workflow design, and the creation of new work.<sup><a href="#ref-39">[39]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-40">[40]</a></sup> Human moral standing and political authorship are not comparative advantages at all. They belong to the welfare function and the theory of legitimacy.

### <span id="appendix-g" class="appendix-anchor"></span>G. The Falsifier Register

*A position that cannot lose is a mood. Here is how this one loses.*

Recorded August 2026. The essay contains a substrate wager and a complementarity thesis. Neither can be dated the way a market can, so the register names the observable that comes first for each claim, where the claim lives, and what breaks.

**G.1 — Digital prosthesis, at the first cortical replacements.** The substrate wager predicts that a digital component can replace neural tissue, preserve the output, and remove the experience that tissue carried (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-b">Appendix B</a>). Cochlear and retinal implants already replace transduction. The gate is cortex. If biological cortex is progressively replaced with digital prostheses while behaviour, introspective continuity, and every credible neural signature of conscious state remain intact, Chalmers's fading-qualia argument wins as an experiment. The wager loses outright.

**G.2 — Convergent indicators, from competing theories.** Current frameworks derive indicators of consciousness from rival theories and update credence rather than verdicts (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-b">Appendix B</a>). If a wholly digital system satisfies convergent, causally tested, difficult-to-game indicators drawn from otherwise competing theories, the wager falls from a position to a minority credence. The wetness analogy would then be a mood.

**G.3 — Subtraction, in living tissue.** Section II and <a class="xref" href="#appendix-c">Appendix C</a> describe dendritic, chemical, interoceptive, and endogenous-field dynamics as the machinery of the subject. If those dynamics can be removed or radically altered while the relevant high-level organisation and conscious experience remain unchanged, the machinery was scaffolding. Section II would describe a brain. It would not describe a mind.

**G.4 — Finite equivalence, from physics and neuroscience together.** <a class="xref" href="#appendix-d">Appendix D</a> already concedes half of this entry: the Bekenstein bound makes every causally relevant distinction in a bounded brain finitely describable. The live half is the second. If neuroscience shows that a finite digital equivalent preserves conscious state across substrate change, representation and occurrence coincide, and the second layer of Appendix D collapses into the first. The mathematics would stand. The wager would not.

**G.5 — Sample efficiency, on the child's budget.** Section II rests the intuition advantage on embodied, active, continuously plastic learning at twenty watts. Data-limited training benchmarks and energy per task are published quantities. If digital systems reach human-like open-ended learning, linguistic sample efficiency, persistent embodied adaptation, and comparable energy cost without reproducing biological dynamics, the advantage becomes history. Twenty watts would be a curiosity rather than an argument.

**G.6 — Conjecture, under scarcity.** Section III and <a class="xref" href="#appendix-e">Appendix E</a> contrast Riemann's compression with the zeta run's bandwidth. If artificial systems repeatedly generate major new conceptual frameworks under novel, underspecified conditions and severe information constraints, compression has a second architecture. The complementarity thesis weakens. The constitution question is untouched.

**G.7 — Authorship, in the market.** Section VII claims that relationship, provenance, responsibility, contestability, and legitimate authorship keep human judgement in the loop (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-f">Appendix F</a>). If AI-only systems durably outperform human–AI systems in domains where those goods are part of what is valued, with no residual demand for human judgement, complementarity was a preference rather than an economics. Standing would remain. It belongs to experience, not to comparative advantage.

Each of these observations would expand the class of architectures that instantiate experience, or redraw the division of work. Experience would remain the source of standing.
