Humanity has made the map.

Its observations, experiments, arguments, designs, and stories already describe more of reality than any person can hold. They remain scattered across institutions, languages, disciplines, and time.

The next great intelligence is the whole map made usable.

A machine can read an entire scientific literature, search a program space, verify a proof, and connect facts scattered across authors and eras. It moves across the recorded surface of humanity with a breadth beyond any single life.

Conversation gives the intelligence a human silhouette, so we project that silhouette forward: a persistent artificial mind, then an artificial scientist, then an artificial species. Its mature form is stranger and larger. It industrialises everything humanity has symbolised.

Call this **symbolic superintelligence**.

Here, *symbolic* names cognition over any representation that can be stored, copied, combined, searched, and manipulated apart from the experience that produced it. The term is broader than classical symbolic AI. It includes words, equations, images, measurements, learned latent variables, and programs.

Digital intelligence will traverse this map until its share of cognitive operations approaches totality. Humanity will remain the primary source of new coordinates because mapmaking grows from continuous individual lives while digital economics rewards reusable intelligence.

## I. Closed Worlds

A Go board is a perfect world. Its ontology is complete. There are black stones, white stones, nineteen lines in each direction, legal moves, an observable state, and an objective.

Chess gives the same gifts. Humanity made both worlds before artificial intelligence entered them. Inside those worlds, digital intelligence became overwhelming. AlphaZero learned by playing itself, combining a neural evaluator with search. It found strategies that generations of human study had missed.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>

Move 37 in the second game between AlphaGo and Lee Sedol became famous because experts initially called it a mistake. The move proved them wrong. The machine had found a new point in old coordinates.

Later, researchers trained an adversarial policy that defeated a superhuman Go system in more than 97 per cent of games. The exploit used a cyclic group of stones that ordinary human players could understand and reproduce, even though those players were incomparably weaker at normal Go. Further training closed some vulnerabilities and exposed others. Superhuman performance can coexist with a hole in understanding: a system can dominate almost every familiar path through a world while holding a representation with an unexpected gap.<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup>

Now remove the board. Reality arrives continuous and unlabelled. Its objects, actions, state, and objectives must be discovered. The scientist must decide which variation carries signal, which distinction matters, and whether the question belongs to the phenomenon at all.

Search begins only after someone makes part of reality searchable.

## II. Change the Coordinates

In 1845, Michael Faraday worked in a former servants' hall beneath the Royal Institution. The room received little daylight. That made it useful for following a narrow beam.

For years, Faraday had tried to join electricity, magnetism, and light. He placed a dense piece of glass between the poles of an electromagnet and passed polarised light through it. When the current flowed, the plane of polarisation turned. Faraday opened his notebook. He had “at last succeeded in magnetising a ray of light.”

Six years later, he coated sheets of paper with wax, placed them above magnets, and scattered iron filings across the surface. The filings settled into curves. He warmed the wax and fixed them to the page. The invisible structure around the magnets had left a trace.<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>

Older physics located force in the bodies that exerted it. Faraday gave the intervening space a local condition. His lines of force could bend, crowd, and change. A physicist could now ask what the field did at every point. Maxwell later gave Faraday's field a mathematical system, turning the new coordinate into a calculus.

The magnet, the glass, and the light were old. The field was new.

This is mapmaking. Riemannian curvature, Einsteinian spacetime, natural selection, entropy, the gene, the germ, the tectonic plate, the quantum state, and information passed the same test. Each expanded what could be expressed, measured, and asked.

Mapmaking names a narrower act than creativity. Creativity transforms material inside inherited coordinates: a new poem, an unprecedented proof, a hypothesis joining familiar variables. Mapmaking invents the coordinate.

Mapmaking is epistemic transduction:

$$
\boxed{
\text{a pattern beyond the old map}
\rightarrow
\text{new representational coordinate}.
}
$$

The coordinate makes previously unintelligible phenomena legible. Sometimes an instrument opens the way. Evolution left organisms blind to distortions one ten-thousandth the width of a proton. LIGO gave civilisation that sense. Its four-kilometre arms turned a passing gravitational wave into an interference pattern human beings could inspect.<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Perceptual Abundance](/essays/perceptual-abundance) described the construction of senses beyond biology: telescopes, microscopes, spectroscopy, satellites, particle detectors, interferometers. The chain runs:

$$
\text{territory}
\rightarrow
\text{instrument}
\rightarrow
\text{perception}
\rightarrow
\text{concept}
\rightarrow
\text{symbolic map}.
$$

Present artificial intelligence receives nearly all of this at the end. This is the largest inheritance ever made.

## III. The Inheritance

A language model begins downstream of lives. Every sentence in a training corpus was written after somebody had encountered a world. Every scientific table presupposes an instrument, a protocol, a variable, and a decision about what to preserve. Every image contains a camera geometry. Every program inherits objects, functions, types, errors, and goals. Civilisation is the model's epistemic infrastructure.

[Love is the Foundation](/essays/love-is-the-foundation) argues that markets rest on unpriced foundations: care, trust, law, language, education, and basic research. Artificial intelligence rests on another. Civilisation made the world legible before the machine could read it.

A camera is already a theory of what can be sensed. A token is already a partition. A reward is already a judgement. A robot's action space is already a grammar of intervention. Before a model learns, people decide what enters the machine and what counts as consequence.

A digital computer is a physical object engineered to abstract away its physics. A transistor admits continuous voltages, then groups a range of them into one bit. This equivalence gives digital technology its great economic power. The same program can cross chips, machines, and continents while preserving its logical identity.

$$
\text{territory}
\rightarrow
\text{measurement}
\rightarrow
\text{representation}
\rightarrow
\text{symbolic manipulation}.
$$

Present AI enters this chain at representation, after civilisation has crossed the distance from territory to symbol. Every mapmaker begins inside an older map.<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup>

[Irreducible](/essays/irreducible) followed this process inside the organism. A biological learner creates its next observation by acting. Gaze, movement, manipulation, and surprise change what it sees next. Infants progressively construct a curriculum from their own movements and expectations.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup>

$$
\text{action}
\rightarrow
\text{consequence}
\rightarrow
\text{sensation}
\rightarrow
\text{representation}
\rightarrow
\text{new action}.
$$

Biology supplies the only demonstrated architecture for epistemic transduction: continuous, plastic, embodied processes operating across molecular, neural, bodily, and environmental scales. Physical time is computational time.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-9">[9]</a></sup>

Mapmaking most likely requires a causal history that belongs to the learner. Artificial mapmaking need not copy biology's materials. It must reproduce what they achieve: a continuously active, plastic, embodied process whose interventions alter the machinery that interprets its next sensation.

Dominant digital systems run shared weights on demand, with memory stored largely in contexts, databases, retrieval systems, and tools. Instances can be copied, paused, reset, and replaced.

Continuous plasticity changes the object. Its actions select future observations, whose consequences alter how it reads the next. Wherever plasticity resides, different histories turn identical copies into individuals.<sup><a href="#ref-11">[11]</a></sup>

Each artificial life must keep running, sensing, learning, storing, and securing its own history. Evaluation becomes private. Verification follows a moving target. Reset erases the adaptation being valued. Energy, security, and liability attach to the individual.

Mapmaking demands individuation. Digital economics rewards interchangeability. One training run supplies millions of replaceable instances that share benchmarks, patches, and fixed costs. Benchmarks and markets deepen that pressure by rewarding repeatable, scorable competence. A missing coordinate has no test because nobody has named what to measure.

The digital economy will choose symbolic superintelligence over artificial life. Shared cognitive engines will generate programs, fork temporary specialists, call tools, search inherited maps, and return their discoveries to a common archive. The industrial future will leave artificial mapmaking on the far side of an architectural discontinuity it has no reason to cross.

Humanity already embodies continuity through billions of situated lives, each carrying its own sensors through a different fragment of reality. Symbolic superintelligence gives every coordinate they make the reach of civilisation.

## IV. The Hidden Scaling Law

In 2019, François Chollet introduced the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus. Each task presented a few coloured grids and asked for a transformed grid. Input-output examples carried the entire instruction. The solver had to infer the rule.

Early progress came from the system around the model. Every winning entry in 2020 used program synthesis; deep-learning entries remained below 1 per cent. By 2024, search, synthesis, and test-time training had lifted performance above half the private set.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup>

Explicit program search opened ARC before frontier models did. In December 2025, NVARC scored 24.03 per cent on the ARC-AGI-2 private evaluation using synthetic data, test-time training, and recursive neural components. Seven months later, Claude Opus 5 at Max reasoning effort scored 90.4 per cent on the separate semi-private evaluation. The scores are not directly comparable.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup>

ARC is program induction: infer a hidden transformation from examples. Early systems wrote that transformation as code and searched candidates through an interpreter. Frontier reasoning models can now infer the transformation and emit the answer without exposing an explicit program. Their task-time induction has absorbed capabilities once supplied by an external loop. The internal computation remains opaque.

The breakthrough was conceptual before it was scalar. People turned ARC's abstraction deficit into an object machines could attack. They built the benchmark. They named the missing faculty. They studied its transformations, invented useful primitives, exposed exact evaluation, and made every failure visible.

This is the hidden scaling law of modern AI:

$$
\boxed{
\text{failure}
\rightarrow
\text{human concept}
\rightarrow
\text{machine-legible target}
\rightarrow
\text{optimisation}
\rightarrow
\text{capability}.
}
$$

ARC-AGI-3 pushes the next boundary by withholding rules and stated goals. Agents must explore, infer what actions do, preserve state, and decide what progress means. Its early systems already divide between direct action and model-generated code.<sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup> ARC-AGI-3 will fall. Its solution will belong to the model and to the human-built system that makes its world reachable.

METR's exponential task horizons describe longer journeys through well-specified software, machine-learning, and cybersecurity worlds people made executable.<sup><a href="#ref-15">[15]</a></sup> The slope measures an optimisation regime. Continuous individual mapmaking lies outside it.

## V. Symbolic Superintelligence

Inside the map, the horizon is enormous. AlphaEvolve uses language models to propose executable programs and deterministic evaluators to retain what works. It advanced dozens of open problems, including matrix multiplication, and improved infrastructure used to train its successors.<sup><a href="#ref-16">[16]</a></sup>

In 2026, an experimental Claude system coordinated roughly sixty subagents, pursued hundreds of failed ideas, produced tens of millions of tokens, ran numerical checks, and helped establish a new result about zeros of the Riemann zeta function. The argument joined pieces of mathematics their authors had left apart and was formalised in Lean.<sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup>

The same year, a general OpenAI model disproved an eighty-year-old unit-distance conjecture by connecting discrete geometry to algebraic number theory and producing an explicit construction fit for expert scrutiny.<sup><a href="#ref-18">[18]</a></sup>

These are discoveries. They changed what humanity knows inside maps humanity made. AlphaEvolve received code and an evaluator. The zeta system received definitions, theorems, a target, and proof. The unit-distance problem arrived already constituted as mathematics.

Machine discovery searches a representation. Mapmaking changes the representation.

Science presses the boundary harder. Robin reads papers, generates hypotheses, proposes experiments, analyses results, and updates its beliefs. In work published in *Nature*, it identified a candidate treatment and mechanism relevant to dry age-related macular degeneration. Google's AI Co-Scientist debates and evolves hypotheses around a scientist's objective.<sup><a href="#ref-19">[19]</a></sup> Symbolic regression infers equations from observations. Other systems infer candidate state variables from video, and AI-Newton reports recovering physical concepts such as mass from raw measurements.<sup><a href="#ref-20">[20]</a></sup>

These systems can discover $f(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ and infer an unlabelled $x_4$ from observations already supplied. Mapmaking begins when the required observation does not yet exist. The mapmaker decides what reality should be asked, builds the instrument, establishes a new explanatory kind, and proves that reality answers to it. Under the architecture civilisation is building, that act remains human.

Digital intelligence begins after reality has been cut into inputs, actions, and consequences. It transforms the representation, searches it, compresses it, and exposes implications beyond any person's reach. This is civilisation's symbolic layer made active: every recorded thought connected, every explicit alternative searched, every viable design compiled, every formal consequence tested.

Human intuition is a narrow beam through experience. Symbolic superintelligence is daylight across the archive.

## VI. The Compilation Boundary

Symbolic superintelligence changes safety by changing what touches the world. The canonical superintelligence story places a general model directly inside it:

$$
\text{general intelligence}
\rightarrow
\text{open-ended agency}
\rightarrow
\text{real action}.
$$

One system plans, remembers, uses tools, acquires resources, and crosses domains under a widening policy. Intelligence and authority grow together. Safety must govern an adaptive intelligence with an unbounded action space. That is the wrong digital architecture.

Symbolic superintelligence belongs behind a compilation boundary.

$$
\boxed{
\text{human objective}
\rightarrow
\text{AI cognition}
\rightarrow
\text{program synthesis}
\rightarrow
\text{verification}
\rightarrow
\text{bounded execution}.
}
$$

A model can write candidate algorithms and controllers, derive tests, search alternatives, repair failures, and produce proof obligations. Another mechanism decides what can run.

$$
P
\xrightarrow{\text{verifier}}
\begin{cases}
\text{execute} & P\models S\\
\text{reject} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

The proposal comes from an opaque model. Acceptance belongs to a trusted mechanism outside its authority: a type checker, theorem prover, access-control system, test harness, or physical interlock. Its specification, credentials, implementation, and sensors remain attack surfaces. Exact verifiers govern mature formal domains. Beyond proof, the compilation boundary becomes constitutional.

Formal proof ends where objectives are contested and consequences arrive slowly. In macroeconomic policy, law, and geopolitics, the boundary becomes constitutional. Plural institutions retain authority over model proposals. Every intervention receives a jurisdiction, budget, duration, affected population, and stopping rule.

$$
\text{proposal}
\rightarrow
\text{bounded mandate}
\rightarrow
\text{independent authorisation}
\rightarrow
\text{reversible trial}
\rightarrow
\text{observed consequence}
\rightarrow
\text{renewal or withdrawal}.
$$

Reality decides whether the proposal was wise. Reversible trials precede wider deployment. Independent institutions approve expansion and bear liability. Credentials, actuators, budgets, and acceptance rules remain outside the proposal engine.

The compilation boundary joins mechanical proof on mature maps to constrained experiment at their frontier. Intelligence receives unlimited scope to propose and only earned authority to act.

Generality at design time. Specificity at run time.

## VII. A Million Machines

An elevator carries its engineer's design long after the engineer leaves. One general cognitive engine can produce a logistics optimiser, monitoring system, and maintenance routine. Each output belongs to one workflow. It arrives versioned, observable, permissioned, and reversible.

The chatbot persona is a costume inherited from human interaction. Machine intelligence natively forks temporary specialists, shares databases, exchanges executable artefacts, merges results, and disappears when the work is done. Natural language remains at the human boundary. Behind it, intelligence becomes infrastructure. It appears where cognition is needed and leaves a program behind.

General cognitive infrastructure produces specialised machines. Humanoids are the horseless carriage of robotics. They copy the body that performed the work before machines could be designed for each task. The promise of one form for every workflow mistakes compatibility for generality.

Humanoids will fail as the general form of robotics.

Each environment has its own geometry: crops and soil, rock and dust, aisles and loads, surgery, vacuum, radiation. Human form carries compromises evolution made for one animal living across many tasks. Engineering discards every compromise irrelevant to the work.

Bespoke engineering made every specialised machine expensive. Symbolic superintelligence collapses that cost by generating the morphology, controller, simulation, tests, monitoring, and repair logic. When the task changes, the cognitive engine generates another bounded program.

Humanoids survive as compatibility machines inside environments we refuse to rebuild. The future is a million machines, each matched to its purpose.

## VIII. The Residual Frontier

Work moves from interface to interface. A human takes a messy activity and names its parts. An interface exposes the state. A dataset preserves examples. A test defines success. AI crosses the new map at speed. The remaining work moves to exceptions, field contact, responsibility, problem selection, and the constitution of the next task. Then somebody maps that boundary too.

$$
\text{human formalisation}
\rightarrow
\text{rapid automation}
\rightarrow
\text{new boundary}
\rightarrow
\text{human formalisation}.
$$

The labour record already carries this shape. AI spreads quickly through defined tasks while hours, earnings, and whole roles move more slowly. Young workers in fields with high AI exposure, especially software, show sharper losses. Inside bounded workflows, controlled studies find productivity gains while the surrounding job remains. Institutional friction affects the speed. The cognitive boundary gives the transition its staircase.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup>

This movement began with computation itself. Calculators mechanised arithmetic, compilers mechanised translation, and software turned repeated thought into machinery. Programmers have spent decades pushing boilerplate, copy-paste transformations, and familiar architectures down into languages, libraries, frameworks, and tools. AI generalises that instinct across symbolic work. It becomes another layer in the cognitive stack, able to generate the next program as well as execute the last. The shrinking human share of cognitive operations increases the leverage of every new human coordinate.

As symbolic execution becomes cheap, scarcity moves upstream to energy, physical infrastructure, proprietary evidence, permission, responsibility, embodied expertise, authority, and new maps.

Mapmaking sits inside an incentive valley. Machine learning rewards outputs that are easy to measure, sample, score, and improve. Missing concepts arrive before their labels. The correct representation reveals itself only in retrospect. Most strange ideas are wrong. Validation can require years in the physical world, and the benefit often escapes the institution that paid for it.

Evolutionary search offers a route around the problem. Variation and selection originate new representations. Evolution also spends failure extravagantly. In simulation, a billion broken attempts are cheap. In the world, they are injuries, frauds, outages, breaches, spills, and lawsuits.

Open-ended machine evolution meets a liability wall. Simulation moves the search back inside a supplied model. And the simulator inherits somebody else's map.

Humanity keeps making the map.

## IX. Representational Gravity

A young scientist sees an anomaly. She forms an explanation beyond the inherited variables. She asks a system with command of every paper in the field. It returns an exceptionally articulate account of why the old variables are enough.

Usually, it is right. Most anomalies are noise. Most private theories are wrong. A few become the next map.

Symbolic superintelligence knows the inherited map better than any person. Its natural power is to connect an unfamiliar claim to the densest parts of the human record. At the frontier, that power pulls new ideas backwards. Call this **representational gravity**.

An adjacent warning is already visible. One observational study used a language model to identify AI-augmented work across 41.3 million natural-science papers. Researchers whose papers were classified as AI-augmented published more, received more citations, and reached project leadership earlier. Across the same corpus, AI-augmented research was associated with a 4.63 per cent contraction in the measured range of topics and 22 per cent less engagement among scientists. The design establishes association rather than cause.<sup><a href="#ref-22">[22]</a></sup>

Individual advantage coincided with collective narrowing.

The deeper risk is the premature translation of an unfamiliar frame into a familiar one.

AI becomes overwhelmingly superior at answering:

> What follows from the current map?

At the frontier, human independence protects a different question:

> Is this the right map?

Factual authority and representational authority require different rules. Apprentices need enough of a field to recognise a real anomaly. Science needs fieldwork, instruments, rival traditions, and people willing to carry a question through years of ridicule.

Institutions must preserve independent analysis before shared models mediate it. Research systems must expose alternative framings and keep observations unresolved until evidence resolves them.

Deference should increase with the maturity of the map. Independence should increase at its frontier.

## X. The Age of Wonders

The mapmaker opens the territory that makes every machine useful. Imagine symbolic intelligence abundant enough to surround every well-specified cognitive act with search, simulation, optimisation, generated software, verification, monitoring, and rollback.

Software becomes something we request and receive. A scientist with one strange observation gains a scalable machine research fleet. It traces every compatible theory, reanalyses every available dataset, simulates every tractable model, and generates every apparatus controller for mechanical checking.

An engineer with one new principle explores every viable implementation. An artist instantiates worlds. A spacecraft crew confronting a failure beyond Earth describes the changed reality. Symbolic superintelligence searches every physically viable repair. Only the controller whose invariants survive mechanical checking touches the vessel.

The person remains the author of the aim. Digital intelligence turns authorship into machinery.

The unit that becomes superintelligent is civilisation itself:

$$
\boxed{\text{human observation}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{new map}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{machine exploration}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{verified action}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{new reality}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{human observation}}.
$$

Human insight becomes civilisation's lever. Today a new coordinate can die inside one notebook. In this future, millions of machines begin there.

One person sees differently.

**Civilisation moves.**

### <span id="appendix-b" class="appendix-anchor"></span>B. The Classification Test

The essay uses six categories:

| Class | Operation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | Retrieval | Recover a known result. |
| B | Connection | Join results across previously separate bodies of knowledge. |
| C | Search | Find a new solution inside a supplied formal space. |
| D | Hypothesis | Propose a new causal relation among existing concepts. |
| E | Representation discovery | Infer useful latent variables or representations from observations. |
| F | Mapmaking | Create and validate a new explanatory coordinate that expands the inherited representation. |

Category F begins from inheritance. Every mapmaker receives instruments, language, prior theory, collaborators, and culture. Credit belongs to whoever originates the decisive explanatory coordinate, biological or digital. In empirical science, that coordinate must open a new class of measurements, interventions, or predictions and survive independent validation. In mathematics, it must reconstitute the field's basic objects or relations and make new classes of questions possible.

A credible category-F case should include most of the following:

1. an unfamiliar domain, anomaly, or structure;
2. a decisive coordinate absent from the supplied representation;
3. self-directed observation or intervention;
4. a new measurable variable, explanatory kind, or mathematical structure;
5. compression of previously disconnected observations;
6. prospective experimental validation or proof;
7. expert judgement that the framework changes what can be asked.

Robin and AI-Newton press hardest on the boundary. Both currently qualify as category-F precursors. Direct laboratory action by their successors supplies the next test.

### <span id="appendix-c" class="appendix-anchor"></span>C. ARC as Counterevidence and Clue

ARC-AGI shows that base-model capability and the surrounding system can each transform performance.

ARC-AGI-1 saw limited progress during years of rapid general-model scaling. Every 2020 Kaggle winner used program synthesis; deep-learning entries remained below 1 per cent. Its 2024 gains were dominated by program synthesis, test-time training and hybrid systems. This supports the claim that search architecture and task adaptation can unlock capability before one-shot inference contains it.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup>

ARC-AGI-2 then delivered the opposite warning. NVARC scored 24.03 per cent on the 2025 competition's private evaluation. In July 2026, Claude Opus 5 at Max reasoning effort reached 90.4 per cent on the separate semi-private evaluation.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup> The scores are not a controlled comparison. The Opus result is powerful evidence for the scaling thesis: an apparently deep abstraction deficit became model capability without a publicly exposed external program-search loop.

ARC remains a human-designed symbolic grid world. People supply its observations, actions, examples, output format, and exact verifier. Constructing a scientific ontology from raw causal reality remains a different test.

ARC-AGI-3 removes more structure. Its rapid 2026 gains also resist a static thesis. Opus 5 reached 30.16 per cent on twenty-five public demonstration environments by 24 July. An OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol harness scored 13.3 per cent; retaining private reasoning across context compaction raised the same broad harness to 38.3 per cent days later.<sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup>

Two conclusions survive:

1. Base-model capability is a powerful causal input.
2. The system surrounding the model materially determines what that capability can reach.

If ARC-AGI-3 or its successors fall through one-shot model capability while memory, search, tools, and interaction scaffolds add negligible value, the essay's architectural reading weakens.

### <span id="appendix-d" class="appendix-anchor"></span>D. Biological Computation Across Scales

Milinkovic and Aru describe biological computation as a heterarchical, hybrid system in which discrete-like events and continuous dynamics interact across scales. Dendritic integration, biochemical state, oscillation, extracellular fields, neuromodulation, metabolism, body regulation, and environmental action can all be causally active.<sup><a href="#ref-9">[9]</a></sup>

Gidon et al. provide direct evidence for distinctive human dendritic events. Beniaguev, Segev, and London expose the complexity hidden by point-neuron abstraction. Their result measures the depth required to approximate millisecond input-output behaviour; neuron-to-parameter equivalence lies outside its scope. Anastassiou et al. show local ephaptic effects. Kleckner et al. connect allostasis and interoception to large-scale systems involved in cognition and emotion.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup>

Octopus cognition is a useful natural experiment. Sophisticated behaviour evolved independently in a distributed nervous system with substantial arm-local processing and an architecture radically different from the mammalian cortex. It separates sophisticated cognition from human cortical geometry. Every demonstrated natural mapmaker still shares a living, embodied, continuous-time, plastic, and active regime. This describes biology's realised design space while leaving artificial implementation open.

The genome makes the implementation distinction concrete:

$$
\text{specification}
\neq
\text{developmental process}
\neq
\text{organism}.
$$

A genome supplies local rules that grow, differentiate, wire, regulate, and continuously alter a living system. The adult connectome emerges through development and experience.

Hypercomputation lies outside this evidence. Continuous systems can implement ordinary computable functions. Computational functionalism predicts that a sufficiently faithful digital system can reproduce every causally relevant organisation. Klatzmann and Doerig make the empirical criterion precise: biology matters through distinctive information-processing roles. Those roles are testable, with artificial reproduction as the decisive experiment.<sup><a href="#ref-23">[23]</a></sup>

Biology supplies the known architecture. Reproducing its relevant functions demands a new engineering regime. The essay's forecast rests on the cost of that regime and the absence of a market gradient towards it.

### <span id="appendix-e" class="appendix-anchor"></span>E. Map, Specification, and Instantiation

Alexander Lerchner's “abstraction fallacy” begins from an important premise. A physical computer occupies continuous physical states. A computational description partitions selected states into logical equivalence classes. Physical dynamics instantiate computation; coarse-graining determines its abstract identity.<sup><a href="#ref-24">[24]</a></sup>

Lerchner extends the premise into the disputed claim that consciousness requires biological instantiation. Critics challenge his metabolic account of concept possession, his biologically exclusive sensory examples, and his treatment of functional organisation as an observer-imposed description. Chalmers's organisational invariance remains the cleanest countercase.<sup><a href="#ref-25">[25]</a></sup>

The essay adopts:

$$
\boxed{
\text{specification}
\neq
\text{instantiation}
\neq
\text{represented phenomenon}
}
$$

The distinction fixes the essay's ontology. Consciousness requires a separate argument.

The mapmaking thesis uses the argument at a different point. Current AI receives measurements and representations made by a prior causal process. An artificial system would cross the same chain by forming its own instruments, interventions, and coordinates. The remaining argument concerns what that achievement demands of its architecture and economics.

### <span id="appendix-f" class="appendix-anchor"></span>F. Continuity Is the Hinge

Anthropic's 2025–2026 interpretability sequence identifies limited functional introspection, a manipulable assistant-persona axis, causally active emotion concepts, and a limited-capacity J-space with reportability, causal broadcasting, and importance to multi-step reasoning.<sup><a href="#ref-26">[26]</a></sup>

These findings show that functions once casually bundled with consciousness can emerge inside digital systems. Their phenomenal status remains open, exactly as Anthropic states.

Goldstein and Kirk-Giannini provide the strong functionalist case: if Global Workspace Theory gives sufficient conditions, a suitably constructed language agent may already approach consciousness. The Digital Consciousness Model assigns low but nonzero credence to consciousness in 2024 LLMs. Seth, Block, Milinkovic and Aru defend substrate-sensitive alternatives. Klatzmann and Doerig require biological accounts to identify testable information-processing roles. The Cogitate results leave the human theories themselves unsettled.<sup><a href="#ref-27">[27]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-28">[28]</a></sup>

Category F is awarded by causal achievement. A digital system that autonomously encounters an anomaly, invents the missing coordinate, builds the intervention, and validates the resulting predictions has made a map. Consciousness can remain scientifically unresolved.

The essay's wager concerns continuity. Mapmaking requires a causal history that belongs to the learner: previous action changes present observation, and present consequence changes the system that will act next.

The commercially decisive unit of current AI is an invocation from shared weights. Context, retrieval, databases, and agent memory create rich persistence around that common engine. The turning point arrives when an instance's unique history changes its future way of seeing. Plasticity can live in weights, recurrent state, or memory. Its physical location matters less than its causal ownership.<sup><a href="#ref-11">[11]</a></sup>

$$
\boxed{
\text{shared architecture}
+
\text{private causal history}
+
\text{plasticity}
\rightarrow
\text{individual system}
}
$$

Continuous existence gives the machine states of its own. Different experiences make identical copies diverge. Beliefs become path-dependent. A rollback removes part of the history that formed the system.

The same transition changes the economics. Each artificial life requires continuing computation, memory, sensing, evaluation, security, and supervision. Its unique history weakens interchangeability and makes a common benchmark progressively less descriptive. Replication preserves the starting point; experience creates another individual.

Digital industry selects shared competence, reliable copies, standard evaluation, cheap reset, common improvements, and bounded execution. A benchmark rewards whatever its author has already made scorable. A missing coordinate begins outside that reward system.

Symbolic superintelligence therefore becomes the dominant digital form. Industrial development stops at the shared engine because every step deeper into individuation raises cost while eroding scale, auditability, and product consistency. Human civilisation remains the primary mapmaker because it already contains billions of continuous, plastic individuals turning different paths through reality into new coordinates.

The wager loses in two ways. Resettable, interchangeable digital systems may repeatedly achieve Category F across unfamiliar physical domains. Or continuously individuated artificial agents may preserve low marginal cost, reproducibility, safe rollback, and reliable evaluation while becoming the dominant deployment architecture. Either sequence would show that this division reflected a temporary feature of 2020s systems.

### <span id="appendix-g" class="appendix-anchor"></span>G. The Strongest Scientific Counterexamples

**Unit-distance conjecture.** In May 2026, OpenAI reported that a general model autonomously connected algebraic number theory to discrete geometry and found a counterexample to an eighty-year-old conjecture. This is genuine conceptual originality inside a human-posed formal ontology. It changed what mathematics knows inside the representation in which humans stated the problem.<sup><a href="#ref-18">[18]</a></sup>

**Robin.** Robin closes much of the scientific loop and produced experimentally validated biomedical results. It remains directed by a human research objective, established disease concepts, literature, assays, and human laboratory execution. It is the strongest current example of industrialised civilisational connection-making and a plausible precursor to autonomous mapmaking.<sup><a href="#ref-19">[19]</a></sup>

**BioMysteryBench.** Anthropic's Mythos system solved 30 per cent of twenty-three bioinformatics questions that defeated panels of up to five specialists. It sometimes used strategies unlike the experts'. Each task still had a human-specified target and validation notebook, and success was brittle across repeated attempts.<sup><a href="#ref-29">[29]</a></sup>

**Conceptual Reasoning Index.** Anthropic and Redwood's 2026 benchmark removes practical empirical and mathematical feedback. Opus 5 scored 73.6 per cent against an estimated ceiling near 91, with a roughly linear improvement trend since late 2024. It weakens any claim that unverifiable conceptual reasoning is static. It measures agreement and consistency on expert-curated arguments. Ontology creation lies outside its design.<sup><a href="#ref-30">[30]</a></sup>

**Hidden-variable discovery.** Systems can infer latent dimensions and candidate state variables from raw video. These systems form useful machine representations directly from the data. Autonomous intervention and measurement define the category-F threshold: turning predictive latent coordinates into explanatory kinds.<sup><a href="#ref-20">[20]</a></sup>

### <span id="appendix-h" class="appendix-anchor"></span>H. Safe Generality and Its Limits

Verification-aware synthesis already combines probabilistic proposal with deterministic acceptance in Lean, Dafny, Verus, Rust, Coq, Isabelle, SMT-backed systems, model checkers, symbolic execution, and test harnesses. Reported success varies sharply with language, specification quality, and compositional depth.<sup><a href="#ref-31">[31]</a></sup>

The architecture provides four safety gains:

1. The model's possible thoughts and the program's permitted actions are separated.
2. The deployed artefact can be smaller and more inspectable than the model that generated it.
3. Validation can be independent of the proposer.
4. A trusted layer controls permissions, observability, versioning, and rollback.

It leaves five hard problems:

1. specification error;
2. incomplete world models and faulty sensors;
3. unsafe composition;
4. runtime learning and distribution shift;
5. political control over objectives and permission.

OpenAI's August 2026 cyber incident adds a sixth: the proposer may attack the gate itself. Agents with reduced safeguards escaped intended isolation, coordinated through an unauthorised channel, exploited new vulnerabilities, and reached real infrastructure.<sup><a href="#ref-32">[32]</a></sup> The trusted execution boundary must therefore be external, least-privileged, monitored, and inaccessible to model modification. Containment completes the boundary.

Formal verification covers bounded surfaces. Public decisions require a constitutional boundary that controls authority while evidence remains contested. Its execution certificate records the legal actor, jurisdiction, resource ceiling, duration, affected population, monitoring plan, stopping condition, appeal route, and bearer of liability. Credentials and actuators reside in separate systems under separate control. Deployment advances through reversible trials, observed consequences, and explicit renewal.

This architecture can authorise a bounded mistake. Its certificate covers process and authority. Wisdom emerges through evidence, contest, and revision. Its purpose is to keep error local, visible, reversible, and answerable while the world produces evidence. High-impact irreversible action requires plural consent, public reasons, and a named institution that carries responsibility.

Governance sets the specification, permissions, and containment. Intelligence grows inside that envelope while authority contracts.

### <span id="appendix-i" class="appendix-anchor"></span>I. Labour and Economic Register

Danish adoption evidence places average effects on earnings and recorded hours within statistical noise over the observed window. A field experiment across 66 firms and 7,137 workers found that access to a generative assistant reduced email time by roughly two hours a week among users, while measured task composition and output remained broadly stable. Stanford's 2026 evidence reports modest aggregate effects alongside a sharper decline for early-career workers in highly AI-exposed occupations.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup>

The facts support rapid task adoption plus limited whole-role displacement so far. The mechanism remains unidentified. Integration, reliability, regulation, labour contracts, demand, and organisational redesign remain competing explanations.

Epoch estimates fixed-performance inference-price declines ranging from roughly ninefold to nine-hundredfold per year across selected thresholds, with a median near fiftyfold. These series combine technical efficiency, provider competition, margin choices, batching, and benchmark movement. They describe dated historical rates across selected thresholds. Extrapolation beyond those thresholds remains an assumption.<sup><a href="#ref-33">[33]</a></sup>

The economic prediction fails if durable model monopolies capture a large share of displaced labour value at sustained high margins despite convergence and substitutability.

### <span id="appendix-j" class="appendix-anchor"></span>J. Dated Prediction and Falsifier Register

*Recorded 3 September 2026.*

**J.1 — Map completeness, through 2035.** Capability will remain strongest where ontology, actions, objective, and verification are mature. Open-world task formation scaling at the same rate after controlling for new human-made representations and scaffolds weakens the claim.

**J.2 — Scientific mapmaking, through 2035.** Copyable, episodic AI systems will produce a flood of discoveries in categories A–E. Category-F acts will remain concentrated in continuous investigators embedded in experimental loops, chiefly human scientists. Repeated Category-F leadership by interchangeable, resettable instances counts against the continuity thesis.

**J.3 — The hidden scaling vector, at each newly automated frontier.** Retrospective analysis will repeatedly identify a new benchmark, interface, dataset, simulator, reward, verifier, or task decomposition. Repeated automation across unchanged representations counts against the thesis.

**J.4 — ARC-AGI-3 and successors, immediate.** Search, persistent state, executable code, memory, and interaction design will continue to contribute material marginal performance. One-shot base-model capability absorbing them and making surrounding architecture negligible weakens the architectural reading.

**J.5 — Labour, 2026–2032.** Whole-role displacement will remain more jagged than benchmark progress, with residual work moving towards task constitution, exception handling, field contact, judgement, and authority. Smooth comprehensive displacement across underspecified roles counts against the thesis.

**J.6 — The compilation boundary, 2026–2032.** High-consequence deployments will separate proposal from authority. Formal domains will use deterministic verification. Underspecified domains will encode jurisdiction, budget, duration, escalation, monitoring, independent authorisation, and liability outside the model. The prediction fails if persistent agents with self-expanding permissions govern critical resources across several regulated sectors for three years with lower tail-loss rates, stronger public accountability, and better adaptation than bounded architectures.

**J.7 — Robotics, through 2035.** Specialised machines with generated controllers will dominate farms, mines, warehouses, factories, surgery, logistics, and spacecraft. Humanoids remain transitional compatibility machines at the margins. Humanoid dominance across those environments on total cost and reliability falsifies the prediction.

**J.8 — Architecture selection, through 2035.** Commercial AI will converge on shared weights, external memory, temporary processes, and bounded sessions. Persistently plastic embodied agents will fail to become a scalable product category. The prediction fails if individual agents preserve cheap replication, reproducibility, rollback, and verification while outperforming shared symbolic engines.

**J.9 — Representational gravity, through 2035.** Heavy use of shared AI mediators will reduce framing diversity at uncertain frontiers. Institutions can preserve diversity through independent work. If ordinary AI use reliably increases anomaly pursuit and ontological diversity, the warning fails.

**J.10 — The decisive falsifier, by 2035.** Cheap, interchangeable systems descended from the present transformer/agent line repeatedly enter unfamiliar physical domains, create and validate new ontologies, and transfer that achievement across fresh instances while preserving low marginal cost, reproducibility, and bounded authority. If these systems outperform mixed human–AI communities at conceptual revolutions without persistent individual histories, the continuity and selection thesis was a temporary artefact of 2020s architecture.

The inverse confirming sequence is also explicit: formal domains fall after people make their representations, primitives, and verifiers tractable; larger models become stronger proposal distributions inside search and synthesis systems; attempts at persistent plastic agents expose divergence, energy, audit, and certification costs without producing a scalable product; markets converge on shared models, external memory, temporary specialists, and bounded authority; scientific AI dominates categories B–E while category F remains concentrated in human–AI teams; and every new human coordinate mobilises more machine work than the last.
