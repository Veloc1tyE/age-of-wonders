Humanity has made a map.

Its observations, experiments, arguments, designs, and stories already describe more of reality than any person can hold. They remain scattered across institutions, languages, disciplines, and time.

The next great intelligence is this whole map made usable.

A machine can read an entire scientific literature, search a program space, verify a proof, and connect facts scattered across authors and eras. It moves across the recorded surface of humanity with a breadth beyond any single life.

Conversation gives the intelligence a human silhouette, so we project that silhouette forward: a persistent artificial mind, then an artificial scientist, then an artificial species. Its mature form is stranger and larger. It industrialises everything humanity has symbolised.

Call this **symbolic superintelligence**.

Here, *symbolic* includes words, equations, images, measurements, learned representations, and programs: anything that can be stored, copied, and worked on apart from the experience that produced it.

Digital intelligence will do most of the work inside the map. Humanity will remain the primary source of new coordinates. Mapmaking grows from individual lives. Digital economics rewards intelligence that can be reused.

<span id="i-mastering-the-world-we-made"></span>

## I. Drawing a World

A Go board is a perfect world. Black stones, white stones, nineteen lines in each direction. The moves are defined, the objective is given, and nothing on the board is hidden.

Chess gives the same gifts. Humanity made both worlds before artificial intelligence entered them. Inside those worlds, digital intelligence became overwhelming. AlphaZero learned by playing itself, combining a neural evaluator with search. It found strategies that generations of human study had missed.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>

Now remove the board. Reality arrives continuous and unlabelled. Its objects, actions, state, and objectives must be discovered. The scientist must decide which variation carries signal, which distinction matters, and whether the question belongs to the phenomenon at all.

Making reality searchable is itself work.

<span id="ii-change-the-coordinates"></span>

## II. Changing the Coordinates

In 1845, Michael Faraday worked in a former servants' hall beneath the Royal Institution. The room received little daylight. That made it useful for following a narrow beam.

For years, Faraday had tried to join electricity, magnetism, and light. He placed a dense piece of glass between the poles of an electromagnet and passed polarised light through it. When the current flowed, the plane of polarisation turned. Faraday opened his notebook. He had “at last succeeded in magnetising a ray of light.”

Six years later, he coated sheets of paper with wax, placed them above magnets, and scattered iron filings across the surface. The filings settled into curves. He warmed the wax and fixed them to the page. The invisible structure around the magnets had left a trace.<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup>

Older physics located force in the bodies that exerted it. Faraday gave the intervening space a local condition. His lines of force could bend, crowd, and change. A physicist could now ask what the field did at every point. Maxwell later gave Faraday's field a mathematical system, turning the new coordinate into a calculus.

The magnet, the glass, and the light were old. The field was new.

This is mapmaking. Natural selection, entropy, the gene, the tectonic plate: each expanded what could be expressed, measured, and asked.

Mapmaking is the part of creativity that changes the terms of understanding. It gives an unfamiliar pattern a form we can think with:

Sometimes the observations are already there. Einstein opened his 1905 relativity paper with the familiar motion of a magnet and a conductor. The phenomenon was the same; the prevailing explanation changed with the chosen frame. He rebuilt the account around relative motion and a definition of simultaneity made with clocks and light.<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>

Sometimes an instrument opens the way. LIGO's four-kilometre arms made the passing distortion of a gravitational wave measurable.<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup> [Perceptual Abundance](/essays/perceptual-abundance) followed these senses beyond biology. Instruments extend observation; concepts change what an observation means. Each can lead the other.

Once established, the new coordinate enters the inheritance. What took a life to see can become a lesson.

<span id="iii-the-inheritance"></span>

## III. The Life Behind the Map

A language model begins downstream of lives. A scientific table presupposes an instrument, a variable, and a decision about what to preserve. A photograph has a viewpoint. A program inherits objects, rules, and goals. Civilisation made the world legible before the model could read it.<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup> [Love is the Foundation](/essays/love-is-the-foundation) argues that markets rest on unpriced care. Artificial intelligence inherits a kind of unpriced care.

Ramanujan began without much. As a teenager he worked through G. S. Carr's compressed collection of mathematical results. A school education and other mathematical books stood behind him; contemporary research was far harder to reach. His notebooks grew into mathematics far beyond Carr.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup> The formulas travelled in letters. But the capacity that found them had grown through years of work.

A small experiment makes the distinction visible. People asked to correct matchstick arithmetic often keep changing the numbers when the solution requires changing an operator. Successful solvers shift their attention towards the signs they had treated as fixed.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup> The marks and the arithmetic were available throughout. What changed was their use.

An investigator learns what to notice, what to revisit, and which failure deserves another attempt. An observation can wait years for the question that makes it matter. The investigator carries experience forward while learning how to read it. A summary preserves what the old question required; a new question can make an omitted distinction decisive. Gaze, movement, memory, and surprise change what the investigator encounters next.<sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup>

Biological computation moves across scales. Neural spikes arise within continuous electrical and chemical dynamics. Rhythms coordinate activity across groups of cells; activity changes connections, and those connections shape later activity.<sup><a href="#ref-9">[9]</a></sup> A brief event can change synaptic strengths and make a neuron respond differently on its next encounter.<sup><a href="#ref-10">[10]</a></sup> Experience changes the machinery that will receive the next experience. [Irreducible](/essays/irreducible) followed this process through the brain, body, and world.

My wager is that this multiscale, continuous organisation is central to mapmaking. Experience reshapes attention and memory together, changing how old observations fit and what becomes available to thought. This is the transductive shift: the investigator gains new terms in which to meet the world.

Computational irreducibility is the barrier. A compact rule can describe a process whose consequences still require the work of unfolding it.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup> Each encounter changes what the next one can teach. I expect fresh mapmaking to depend on this work. We can inherit a discovery while still having to grow into the next.

<span id="iv-the-hidden-scaling-law"></span>

## IV. Making the Frontier Learnable

In 2019, François Chollet introduced the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus. Each task presented a few coloured grids and asked for a transformed grid. The solver had to infer the rule from examples.

Early winners searched for programs that produced the right transformation. Later systems combined that search with models that learned during the task.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup> Frontier models absorbed abilities once supplied by the machinery around them.<sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup>

People made abstraction into something a machine could practise. They built the benchmark, invented useful operations, and made failures visible. One recurring route to capability is:

A capability first assembled around a model becomes part of the shared engine. Models increasingly build parts of the scaffold themselves.

ARC-AGI-3 pushed further: agents explored unfamiliar games and discovered what counted as progress. When its reasoning could accumulate across calls, Astra went much further. It built algebraic models of the games and, with code tools, wrote planners and small libraries of its own.<sup><a href="#ref-15">[15]</a></sup> Experience gave the shared engine new ways to understand the world it had entered.

METR's growing task horizons show longer journeys through software and other worlds people have made executable.<sup><a href="#ref-16">[16]</a></sup> The frontier keeps moving. The question is who maps the next part of reality.

<span id="v-symbolic-superintelligence"></span>

## V. Daylight Across the Archive

Inside the map, the horizon is enormous. AlphaEvolve uses language models to propose executable programs and deterministic evaluators to retain what works. It advanced dozens of open problems, including matrix multiplication, and improved infrastructure used to train its successors.<sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup>

In 2026, an experimental Claude system turned a broad invitation to investigate the Riemann hypothesis into a new result about zeros of the zeta function. It chose an intermediate target and joined ideas its mathematical sources had left apart; the argument was formalised in Lean.<sup><a href="#ref-18">[18]</a></sup> A general OpenAI model supplied another challenge to easy boundaries: a counterexample to an eighty-year-old unit-distance conjecture, reached by changing how algebraic number theory entered the geometry.<sup><a href="#ref-19">[19]</a></sup>

These systems made conceptual contributions. Inheriting a problem disqualifies neither a machine nor Ramanujan. Credit belongs to whoever opens the new way of seeing.

Science presses harder. Robin chose experiments and revised hypotheses from laboratory results, identifying an eye-disease treatment candidate tested in cells.<sup><a href="#ref-20">[20]</a></sup> AI-Newton learned physical concepts through a growing record of simulated experiments. Other systems recover useful state variables from video.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup> Representation discovery, experimental design, and the construction of tests already belong to machine intelligence.

Imagine AI making almost every sufficiently specified task cheap. Its reach then depends on the work that makes a task possible:

<Callout type="info">

A program can satisfy a verifier.

An investigator can establish whether that verifier captures the phenomenon.

A mapmaker can create the distinction that makes a new question testable.

</Callout>

One system may perform all three; their costs need not fall together.

I expect the widest abundance in the first of these, with growing machine contributions to the other two. Digital intelligence will connect the archive, transform its representations, and expose consequences beyond any person's reach. Mapmaking gives that power somewhere new to go.

Human intuition is a narrow beam through experience. Symbolic superintelligence is daylight across the archive.

</PullQuote>

## VI. Unlimited Thought, Bounded Action

Symbolic superintelligence changes safety by changing what touches the world. A general model can propose a design, search alternatives, and repair its mistakes. Another mechanism decides what can run.

<ConceptFlow steps={[
  'human objective',
  'AI cognition',
  'program synthesis',
  'verification',
  'bounded execution',
]} />

Call this the compilation boundary. The model produces a program; a separate system checks and authorises it. Tests sample its behaviour. Proofs establish claims under stated assumptions. Physical interlocks limit what it can touch. The gates remain outside the model's control.

Where objectives are contested and consequences arrive slowly, the boundary becomes constitutional. A proposal for a city or an economy needs a jurisdiction, budget, duration, and stopping rule. Independent institutions approve a trial, observe what follows, and decide whether to continue. Credentials, actuators, and acceptance rules remain outside the proposal engine (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-h">Appendix H</a>).

Intelligence receives unlimited scope to propose and only earned authority to act.

Generality at design time. Specificity at run time.

## VII. A Million Machines

An elevator carries its engineer's design long after the engineer leaves. One general cognitive engine can produce a logistics optimiser, monitoring system, and maintenance routine. Each does a specific job under explicit permissions. Its actions can be watched; its program can be inspected and replaced.

The chatbot persona is a costume inherited from human interaction. Machine intelligence natively forks temporary specialists, shares databases, exchanges executable artefacts, merges results, and disappears when the work is done. Natural language remains at the human boundary. Behind it, intelligence becomes infrastructure. It appears where cognition is needed and leaves a program behind.

Humanoids are the horseless carriage of robotics. They copy the body that performed the work before machines could be designed for each task.

Each environment has its own geometry: crops and soil, rock and dust, aisles and loads, surgery, vacuum, radiation. Human form carries compromises evolution made for one animal living across many tasks. Engineering discards every compromise irrelevant to the work.

Bespoke engineering made every specialised machine expensive. Symbolic superintelligence collapses that cost by generating the morphology, controller, simulation, tests, monitoring, and repair logic. When the task changes, the cognitive engine generates another bounded program.

Humanoids survive as compatibility machines inside environments we refuse to rebuild.

<PullQuote>

The future is a million machines, each matched to its purpose.

</PullQuote>

## VIII. We Are Wanderers

A human takes a messy activity and names its parts. An interface exposes the state. A dataset preserves examples. A test defines success. AI crosses the new map at speed. The remaining work moves to exceptions, field contact, responsibility, problem selection, and the constitution of the next task. Then somebody maps that boundary too.

  'insight',
  'rapid automation',
  'new boundary',
  'insight',
]} />

A digital investigator can copy what it has learned and send a thousand descendants down different paths. A single valuable discovery can pay for many failures. The next frontier asks what this inheritance has prepared them to see.

  insight="The cost that matters is what returns at the next frontier."
/>

Fresh frontiers repeatedly require new adaptation. An unexpected result changes the next experiment. A rival explanation sends attention back to an overlooked detail. The investigator must grow into the question, carrying its significance across encounters whose consequences arrive later.

Weather offers a warning about the cost of holding a changing world in a model. Small errors grow until a forecast loses the storm. In high-resolution weather-model experiments, tenfold smaller initial errors bought only three to five more days of useful prediction.<sup><a href="#ref-22">[22]</a></sup> A longer horizon demanded a much more accurate starting picture.

A brain carries experience through currents, rhythms, and changes in its own structure. My wager is that their contribution to discovery resists economical digital reduction. Preserving it across longer histories requires exponentially more digital work. Living tissue sustains that capacity where a digital reproduction becomes prohibitively costly. A simulation can be copied; every copy still has to be run.

People learn to notice while earning a living, caring for one another, and making things. The preparation for a discovery can begin long before anyone knows the question. Biology turns this unplanned variety into a living capacity for new maps. Building that capacity economically draws artificial investigators towards the organisation of a life: persistent plasticity, individual history, and adaptation across timescales. The past remains at work in the machinery that meets the next moment.

Science already improves the means of its own improvement. Discoveries become instruments; instruments open further discoveries. Yet sustaining the computer chip's roughly two-year doubling rhythm took an eighteenfold increase in research effort between 1971 and 2014, measured through wage-adjusted spending.<sup><a href="#ref-23">[23]</a></sup> The machines became exponentially more capable. The rhythm of improvement held its pace.

I therefore predict no hard takeoff for digital intelligence: no rapid, self-sustaining passage from expert competence to overwhelming general research ability. The cost of sustaining new mapmaking will bind before that transition. Digital intelligence's great commercial form will be the advanced compiler, turning shared knowledge into a million useful machines. Artificial investigators can prosper beside it wherever the work fits their reach, sharing their discoveries with the common engine.

As symbolic work gets cheaper, its prerequisites matter more: energy, infrastructure, evidence, permission, and someone who knows what to ask. Humanity already contains billions of lives, each moving through a different fragment of reality. Symbolic superintelligence gives what they find the reach of civilisation.

## IX. Representational Gravity

A young scientist sees an anomaly. She forms an explanation beyond the inherited variables. She asks a system with command of every paper in the field. It returns an exceptionally articulate account of why the old variables are enough.

Usually, it is right. Most anomalies are noise. Most private theories are wrong. A few become the next map.

Shared models make the inherited map extraordinarily persuasive. When many investigators consult the same engines, their different starting points can converge before any independent test. Call this **representational gravity**.

In a randomised story-writing experiment, access to AI ideas improved judged creativity while making stories more alike.<sup><a href="#ref-24">[24]</a></sup> Better individual answers can coexist with fewer directions explored. At the scientific frontier, a departure may need years of independent attention before its value becomes clear.

“What follows from this map?” and “Is this the right map?” need different habits of trust. Apprentices need enough of a field to recognise a real anomaly. Science needs fieldwork, instruments, rival traditions, and people willing to carry an unresolved question.

Institutions should preserve independent analysis before shared models mediate it, then use those models to strengthen rival explanations and design discriminating tests. An artificial investigator that sustains and validates an unfamiliar frame belongs among the departures we should protect.

<PullQuote>

Deference should increase with the maturity of the map. Independence should increase at its frontier.

</PullQuote>

<span id="x-the-age-of-wonders"></span>

## X. Civilisation Becomes the Superintelligence

The mapmaker opens the territory that makes every machine useful. Imagine enough symbolic intelligence to explore every well-posed question, test each promising answer, and build the tools to act on it.

Software becomes something we request and receive. A scientist with one strange observation gains a machine research fleet. It traces compatible theories, reanalyses available datasets, simulates candidate models, and writes instrument controllers for independent checking.

An engineer with one new principle explores designs no team had time to test. An artist builds worlds. A spacecraft crew confronting a failure beyond Earth describes the changed reality. The system searches for repairs. Independent checks and physical interlocks determine what may touch the vessel.

The person remains the author of the aim. Digital intelligence turns authorship into machinery.

The unit that becomes superintelligent is civilisation itself:

$$
\boxed{\text{human observation}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{new map}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{machine exploration}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{verified action}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{new reality}}
\rightarrow
\boxed{\text{human observation}}.
$$

Human insight becomes civilisation's lever. Today a new coordinate can die inside one notebook. In this future, millions of machines begin there.

One person sees differently.

**Civilisation moves.**

### <span id="appendix-b" class="appendix-anchor"></span>B. The Classification Test

The essay uses *symbolic* for learned representations as well as explicit symbols, extending beyond classical symbolic AI. Its six categories distinguish what an investigator does with that inheritance:

| Class | Operation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | Retrieval | Recover a known result. |
| B | Connection | Join results across previously separate bodies of knowledge. |
| C | Search | Find a new solution inside a supplied formal space. |
| D | Hypothesis | Propose a new causal relation among existing concepts. |
| E | Representation discovery | Infer useful latent variables or representations from observations. |
| F | Mapmaking | Create and validate a new explanatory coordinate that expands the inherited representation. |

Category F begins from inheritance. Every mapmaker receives instruments, language, prior theory, collaborators, and culture. Credit belongs to whoever originates the decisive explanatory coordinate, biological or digital. A human may supply the anomaly or broad objective; a laboratory may execute the experiment. Existing observations may suffice. None of these arrangements settles the attribution.

A Category-F claim must establish three things:

1. **Origin.** The decisive coordinate was created by the credited investigator rather than supplied in its prompt, training materials, collaborator's intervention, or experimental design. Record the inheritance and division of work.
2. **Explanatory change.** The coordinate introduces an explanatory kind, object, or relation that opens a new class of questions, measurements, interventions, or predictions. A useful latent encoding or a new route to a fixed answer earns its actual achievement; Category F requires this further change.
3. **Validation.** Independent evidence or proof supports the coordinate and at least one consequence that distinguishes it from the prior account. In empirical work, seek prospective tests where possible. In mathematics, demonstrate both soundness and the new questions the structure makes possible.

Apply these criteria to human and machine work before deciding who succeeded. Expert review evaluates the explanatory contribution, not whether its author resembles a scientist. A complete causal record may justify shared credit. A new instrument, a robot body, phenomenal consciousness, and sole authorship are optional.

Rediscovering a coordinate already known to humanity can be a strong test of independent learning if exposure is controlled. Keep that result separate from historically new Category F. Ordinary insight, including much of Ramanujan's work, can illuminate the developmental mechanism without meeting the standard for conceptual revolution. Once a machine passes, the category stays where it was.

### <span id="appendix-c" class="appendix-anchor"></span>C. ARC as Counterevidence and Clue

ARC-AGI shows that base-model capability and the surrounding system can each transform performance.

ARC-AGI-1 saw limited progress during years of rapid general-model scaling. Every 2020 Kaggle winner used program synthesis; deep-learning entries remained below 1 per cent. Its 2024 gains were dominated by program synthesis, test-time training and hybrid systems. This supports the claim that search architecture and task adaptation can unlock capability before one-shot inference contains it.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup>

ARC-AGI-2 then delivered the opposite warning. NVARC scored 24.03 per cent on the 2025 competition's private evaluation. In July 2026, Claude Opus 5 at Max reasoning effort reached 90.4 per cent on the separate semi-private evaluation.<sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup> The evaluations used different sets. The Opus result is powerful evidence for the scaling thesis: an apparently deep abstraction deficit became model capability without a publicly exposed external program-search loop.

ARC remains a human-designed symbolic grid world. People supply its observations, actions, examples, output format, and exact verifier. The model's own representations can be substantial achievements. Category F asks the further question of whether a new explanatory coordinate changes what can be investigated.

ARC-AGI-3 asked agents to explore unfamiliar games. Its 2026 results show gains from both stronger models and changes to the systems around them:<sup><a href="#ref-15">[15]</a></sup>

| Model | Evaluation and setup | Score |
|---|---|---|
| Opus 5 | 25 public demonstration environments, by 24 July; High reasoning effort | 30.16% |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | Public set; official harness | 13.3% |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | Same public set; Responses API harness, retained reasoning and compaction | 38.3% |
| Astra | 55 semi-private environments, 2 September; standard harness, Max effort | 62.71% |
| Astra | Same semi-private set; provider adapter, Max effort | 98.55% |
| Astra | Same semi-private set; provider adapter, High effort; best observed run | 99.95% |

Across the 167 game–effort pairs both Astra harnesses solved, the adapter used 49 per cent fewer tokens and finished 3.66 times faster in aggregate. Keeping the model's reasoning available changed how far it could go.

Astra created compact symbolic world models and its own algebraic shorthand from interaction. In a separate sandboxed harness it generated parsers, planners, search algorithms, and game-specific software libraries. This is strong endogenous representation discovery inside a supplied world. ARC Prize defines the scope of these results: the environments have deterministic, closed-ended mechanics and goals. The perceptual interface, legal actions, score, and validation still come from the benchmark.

1. Base-model capability is a powerful causal input.
2. The system surrounding the model materially determines what that capability can reach.
3. A model can create powerful working representations inside a supplied world without originating the perceptual interface, action grammar, and criterion that constituted the task.

If ARC-AGI successors fall through one-shot model capability while memory, search, tools, and interaction scaffolds add negligible value, the essay's architectural reading weakens.

### <span id="appendix-d" class="appendix-anchor"></span>D. Biological Computation Across Scales

Experience changes how the next experience is received:

$$
\text{encounter}
\rightarrow
\text{changed memory and plasticity}
\rightarrow
\text{changed attention and representation}
\rightarrow
\text{next encounter}.
$$

In the matchstick experiment, twenty-four people knew the arithmetic and could see the complete display. Twenty-three solved a problem by changing a value; nine solved one that required changing an operator. Successful solvers shifted their attention. The task demanded a new use of familiar marks.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup>

Bittner and colleagues showed how one encounter could change a neuron's next response. A dendritic calcium plateau could rapidly create a place field in a mouse hippocampal neuron. Inputs arriving seconds around the event gained strength; on later traversals the neuron fired predictively. Their model linked a lingering trace of recent input with a signal from the plateau to determine which connections strengthened. Reproducing the recorded responses required those signals to last. The study concerns spatial learning; the trace's molecular pathway remained unresolved.<sup><a href="#ref-10">[10]</a></sup>

Milinkovic and Aru propose that consciousness depends on hybrid discrete–continuous computation, coupled scales, metabolic constraints, and mutual changes between neural activity and structure. They allow synthetic implementations and leave the cognitive benefits of continuous dynamics to experiment.<sup><a href="#ref-9">[9]</a></sup> This essay wagers that the same organisation helps an investigator create new explanatory coordinates—a claim to test through discovery.

The proposed mechanism is learning what to keep and what to change. Fast activity brings present input together with remembered context. Slower physical changes preserve useful combinations and alter later learning. An investigator acts on an interpretation; the consequences reshape both the representation and what it attends to next. The biological wager is that this coupling makes mapmaking economical and resilient. Metabolic limits shape how the system allocates its resources.

Dendritic experiments and neuron-emulation models reveal active mechanisms hidden by a point-neuron description.<sup><a href="#ref-25">[25]</a></sup> In Beniaguev and colleagues' study, a five-to-eight-layer temporal convolutional network approximated a detailed cortical-neuron model's input–output behaviour. Removing NMDA conductances greatly simplified the approximation. The learned surrogate ran about two thousand times faster than the detailed simulation. Its size shows what one successful approximation used. The study reveals both complexity within a single neuron and the scope for compressing its simulation.

Timing also crosses cell boundaries. Anastassiou and colleagues applied weak oscillating electric fields to rat cortical slices. The fields shifted spike timing even when synaptic transmission was blocked, coordinating neighbouring cells without raising their firing rates.<sup><a href="#ref-26">[26]</a></sup> Here, locking into phase has a concrete meaning: cells fire at related points in an electrical rhythm. Together, these experiments show mechanisms that change which sequences cells distinguish and when they respond. The essay wagers that preserving their contribution to learning across longer histories imposes a growing digital cost.

Across six mammalian species, whole-brain glucose use was approximately proportional to neuron count, over a roughly twelve-hundredfold range in neurons.<sup><a href="#ref-27">[27]</a></sup> Biology added neural machinery at roughly constant metabolic cost per neuron. Does that economy extend to creating new concepts?

A small brain can also acquire a relationship that travels beyond its training examples. Honeybees trained to match odours transferred matching to colours after eighty training visits.<sup><a href="#ref-28">[28]</a></sup> This is a modest example of transferable learning. The candidate anomaly lies in the larger combination: transfer, continuing adaptation, and physical economy.

Moravec contrasted success in formal problem-solving with the difficulty of perception and movement.<sup><a href="#ref-29">[29]</a></sup> An ordinary cat or dog combines sensing, movement, learning, and bodily regulation throughout its day. Compare that continuing bundle, including unfamiliar situations, on common tasks. Count the animal's full metabolism and development, and the machine's training, inference, sensors, actuators, and human support.

The sharper test concerns evidence that becomes important only later. Shorten the window linking recent input to plateau-driven plasticity while checking that immediate cue learning and recall survive. Delayed learning should suffer, then recover when the window is restored. Compare fine-grained dynamics with tuned digital rules that preserve the trace, and with replay. Give each the same experience, test on tasks withheld during tuning, and count computation, memory, and energy. The stronger wager predicts that coarse replacements lose useful adaptation as relevance changes, or pay more to recover it. A cheaper digital replacement retaining the benefit defeats the claimed need for finer continuous detail. Selective loss and recovery would support the coupling's contribution; its role in scientific reframing remains to be tested.

A faithful functional duplicate would duplicate functional epistemic capacities. Success on selected tasks establishes a narrower equivalence. The question is what the fidelity needed for discovery costs.<sup><a href="#ref-30">[30]</a></sup>

### <span id="appendix-e" class="appendix-anchor"></span>E. Map, Specification, and Instantiation

Alexander Lerchner argues that abstract computation depends on a mapmaker who assigns meaningful discrete states to physical processes. Producing a simulation and producing the experience it represents are different achievements. He concludes that syntactic organisation alone cannot create consciousness, while allowing artificial consciousness with an appropriate physical constitution.<sup><a href="#ref-31">[31]</a></sup>

Lerchner's argument turns on whether meaningful abstraction requires someone who experiences. Chalmers starts from a rival view: preserve the right fine-grained functional organisation and experience follows.<sup><a href="#ref-32">[32]</a></sup> These are competing accounts of what consciousness requires. The cost of developing an investigator needs its own evidence.

The distinction is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{specification}
\neq
\text{instantiation}
\neq
\text{represented phenomenon}
}
$$

An equation can describe a developmental process without performing it. The essay's wager is that mapmaking depends on adaptive physical dynamics whose discovery-relevant effects resist economical digital reduction. If preserving those effects across longer histories requires exponentially increasing computational work, biology retains a practical advantage even where digital reproduction remains possible.

Copying a mature state preserves a starting point. Every active copy still pays for the dynamics that sustain its capacity to discover. This continuing cost is the proposed barrier. Biological computationalism supplies reasons to investigate it; the scaling relationship is the essay's additional hypothesis. A digital investigator that sustains new mapmaking at competitive total cost would defeat this barrier. Every investigator receives credit for its discoveries, whatever its physical implementation.

Phenomenal acquaintance—the presence of experience to its subject—and publicly demonstrable discovery are distinct claims. A proposed causal bridge would run from experienced surprise or unresolved tension to attention, persistence, and representational revision. Evidence for those functional effects alone leaves the contribution of felt experience unsettled. Category F therefore rests on the discovery record, while consciousness remains a separate question. Functional introspection and workspace-like organisation in digital models make that separation increasingly important.<sup><a href="#ref-33">[33]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-34">[34]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-35">[35]</a></sup>

### <span id="appendix-f" class="appendix-anchor"></span>F. What Development Costs

Recording a concept, discovering it, checking it, and developing its originator are four different jobs. A concept that fits on one line can take years to find. Another investigator may take a faster path.

#### What experience leaves behind

A notebook keeps what its writer thought mattered. The next question may need what was left out. Brains make selections too. Their proposed advantage is learning what to keep and how to revisit old experience when its significance changes. Even a complete record needs someone who can find a new organisation within it. The wager is that this work repeatedly changes how the investigator learns next.

| Source of difficulty | What could remain after faster inference | What could remove or amortise it |
|---|---|---|
| Missing information | A required observation has yet to be made. | Better sensors, shared observations, or a successful inference from existing data. |
| Sequential experiments | The next useful intervention depends on the previous result. | Parallel branches, faster apparatus, or a sufficiently faithful simulator. Physical delay applies to humans too. |
| Internal restructuring | Attention, concepts, or search methods must change despite available evidence. | Better algorithms, in-context learning, replay, distillation, or transferable representations. |
| Long-term credit | The value of an early choice becomes visible much later. | Improved learning rules and world models; delayed feedback still needs to arrive. |
| Individual development | A history changes competence and the ability to learn the next task. | Copying the full acquired state or sharing a learning method that transfers across fresh frontiers. |
| Continuing dynamics | Each active investigator must sustain the processes that keep its experience useful as questions change. | Cheaper algorithms, sufficient approximations, or hardware that performs the relevant dynamics directly. Copying state alone leaves the running cost. |

The same observations can fit different causal explanations. With suitable assumptions, interventions can distinguish them. Causal representation learning studies how a system can recover the hidden causes and reuse what it learns across tasks.<sup><a href="#ref-36">[36]</a></sup> Its results specify conditions under which that recovery and transfer work.

In Dohare and colleagues' image-classification and reinforcement-learning experiments, continued training sometimes reduced the ability to learn. Regularisation and resetting low-utility units kept the networks plastic.<sup><a href="#ref-37">[37]</a></sup> Learning history created a problem that a different learning rule could overcome.

Follow what learning leaves behind: a changed memory, tool library, world model, context, or weights. Useful adaptation can survive a pause and coexist with shared machinery, as Astra's retained reasoning shows.<sup><a href="#ref-15">[15]</a></sup> Proposals for lifelong agents build on such persistence.<sup><a href="#ref-38">[38]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-39">[39]</a></sup> Appendix D asks how much of biology's continuous, multiscale dynamics a learner must reproduce to retain their benefits.

Count preparation, continuing operation, adaptation to a fresh frontier, and validation separately. Human preparation includes childhood, education, practical experience, care, and research support; machine preparation includes architecture, training, data, tools, and human assistance. Record biological and cultural inheritance, and show how shared investments and copied state spread costs. Both kinds of investigator face experimental delays and validation. A durable division requires a cost difference after these common burdens are counted. Changed competence can require renewed certification; permission and containment remain separate choices.

The strongest rival is a research enclave: expensive artificial investigators develop new capacities, then distribute discoveries and useful state to cheap engines. High-value discoveries could fund the enclave even if its agents surrender some interchangeability. This defeats the essay's biological division if the enclave leads mapmaking; it leaves the abundance and bounded-execution arguments intact.

Test the remaining bridge across successive unfamiliar frontiers. Compare intact investigators, full-state clones, fresh engines given compressed reports or replay, and systems given distilled shared improvements. Include human and mixed teams. Keep access to evidence and tools comparable; vary retained adaptation. Count preparation, compute, elapsed interaction, human assistance, failed experiments, and validation. Assess who made the coordinate using Appendix B, then test transfer to the next frontier. An advantage beyond additional information or computation supports the developmental mechanism. Cheap, broad transfer with inexpensive continuing operation can defeat its economic consequence.

#### What the rule cannot skip

Knowing a process's rules and starting state can leave substantial work to reach its outcome. Computational irreducibility names the limit where no general method substantially shortcuts that unfolding.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup> Wolfram conjectures that this is widespread, even among simple rules. Applying it to biological discovery requires a further argument.

Moore and Nordahl proved a result for two lattice-gas models: their finite-time prediction problems are P-complete. Unless P equals NC, no general algorithm predicts them in polylogarithmic parallel time using polynomial resources.<sup><a href="#ref-40">[40]</a></sup> This worst-case limit on parallel prediction permits substantial speedups. Its relevance to discovery turns on what an investigator must preserve.

A useful shortcut can change what it predicts. Israeli and Goldenfeld constructed predictable coarse descriptions of complex cellular automata, including a computationally universal rule.<sup><a href="#ref-41">[41]</a></sup> By preserving a selected aspect of the behaviour, the simpler account leaves much of the underlying process behind.

Mapmaking changes which aspect matters. A small difference can leave today's answer unchanged while altering what tomorrow's experience teaches. The biological candidate is the coupling of fast activity, lingering traces, and slower changes in plasticity: an encounter changes both a representation and how it can later change. A reduction must preserve this capacity across questions that arise after it was built. It must retain the relevant differences, recover them, or learn replacements. My wager is that this adaptive work repeatedly resists a general cheap shortcut. Adaptive compression is the strongest rival; it belongs in the test.

Irreducibility concerns work that must be done. The scaling wager concerns the cost of doing it. Digital systems can contain irreducible processes too, and faster hardware can accelerate necessary steps. Biology's proposed advantage is economical execution. Exponential digital cost requires a further relationship between fidelity and expense.

#### Buying a longer horizon

How much experience can a simulator replace? Weather gives a concrete case in which a longer forecast demands a much more accurate starting picture. While small errors grow approximately exponentially, an error of initial size $\varepsilon_0$ becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon(t)&\approx\varepsilon_0 e^{\lambda t},\\[0.6em]
H&\approx\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\frac{\varepsilon_*}{\varepsilon_0}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\lambda>0$ is the error-growth rate, $\varepsilon_*$ is the largest useful error, and $H$ is the forecast horizon in the same time units as $1/\lambda$. Extending the horizon by a fixed interval requires another fixed-factor reduction in initial error. In Zhang and colleagues' high-resolution model experiments, tenfold smaller initial perturbations extended useful deterministic midlatitude weather forecasts by roughly three to five days.<sup><a href="#ref-22">[22]</a></sup>

What would make that accuracy expensive? Consider a particular grid-based numerical method. Let $s$ be spatial refinement relative to a reference mesh, $q>0$ its error-convergence exponent, and $r>0$ its computational-cost exponent. Let $R$ count operations per simulated second, with reference rate $R_0$. Over a forecast lasting $\tau$ seconds, suppose the method's error and work scale over the range studied as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon(s,\tau)&\approx A s^{-q}e^{\lambda\tau},\\[0.6em]
R(s)&=R_0s^r,\\[0.6em]
R_{\mathrm{req}}(\tau)&\propto e^{(r\lambda/q)\tau}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $A$ sets the error scale. Holding the tolerated error at $\varepsilon_*$ gives the third relation: longer forecasts require finer resolution and more work per simulated second. At real-time execution and fixed energy per operation, power rises in the same proportion. For a three-dimensional grid whose time step shrinks in proportion to mesh spacing, $r=4$: twice the spatial resolution means sixteen times the work per simulated second.

This illustrative model assumes a particular method and error target. For mapmaking, the required fidelity is whatever lets the investigator keep discovering. The wager is that economical approximations face a comparable accuracy–cost trade-off even as numerical methods, abstractions, and hardware improve.

An investigator's *adaptive horizon* is the span across which an experience can still change a later interpretation or discovery. A different train of thought can still produce a valid discovery. The wager concerns lost capacity: omitted interactions make a useful distinction harder to learn when later experience brings it into view. Biology's proposed advantage is sustaining and revising those sensitivities within a viable physical budget.

Within a specified family of investigations, hold operating speed and reliability fixed. Let $h$ measure the temporal dependencies the investigator successfully integrates, and suppose the power required by competitive digital implementations grows as

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_D(h)&=P_0 e^{\kappa(h-h_0)},\\[0.6em]
h_{\max}&=h_0+\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\frac{P_{\max}}{P_0}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $P_0$ is the power in watts needed at a reference horizon $h_0$, $\kappa>0$ is the rate at which that cost rises, and $P_{\max}$ is available power. If $h$ is measured in days, $\kappa$ has units of inverse days. The second relation gives the longest supported horizon within the range where the assumed scaling holds. Each multiplication of power buys another fixed interval of adaptive reach. Every running copy pays the cost.

The exponent and applicable range await measurement across efficient approximations and competing digital architectures. Slower execution can reduce required power. Practical exclusion requires power, elapsed time, or total expense to become prohibitive at the capacity the investigation needs, while biology sustains it within a viable metabolic and developmental budget.

A simulation can lose the path of a particular storm while retaining useful climate statistics. Investigators can likewise use stable relations, abstractions, feedback, and interventions without forecasting every event. Their adaptive reach can extend beyond the tokens immediately available in a context window. Lengthen dependencies while controlling information, task difficulty, and opportunities to act. Measure which implementation keeps discovering, and what it costs. A digital implementation that preserves the benefit at competitive running cost defeats the proposed fidelity barrier.

#### What powers the next discovery

Discoveries improve the means of making discoveries. Bloom and colleagues estimated that effective semiconductor research effort rose eighteenfold from 1971 to 2014 while sustaining an approximately steady chip-density doubling rhythm.<sup><a href="#ref-23">[23]</a></sup> They measured input as research spending deflated by skilled-worker wages, and output as proportional technical progress. Self-improvement coexisted with declining research productivity. Artificial research could change the institutions, training and experimental constraints behind that history.

Digital efficiency gains are substantial too. Ho and colleagues estimated that the training compute needed for fixed language-model benchmark performance halved roughly every eight months over 2012–2023. They warn that the gains depend on scale and cannot simply be extrapolated to arbitrarily small compute budgets.<sup><a href="#ref-42">[42]</a></sup> Cheap execution of an established capability and costly development of a new one can therefore coexist. The relevant curve measures total energy per validated frontier advance, after reusable improvements have been shared.

Available power turns that energy requirement into time. For a specified capability transition, let $E_*$ be the required joules, $P_{\max}$ the maximum available watts, and $\tau_*$ the duration of its necessary sequential work. Then

$$
T_*\geq\max\left(\frac{E_*}{P_{\max}},\tau_*\right).
$$

The energy includes investigation, failed experiments, adaptation, and validation. The bound converts a given energy requirement into a minimum time. Today's datacentres consume a small share of world electricity; the immediate constraints are deliverable power, chips, cooling, and experimental capacity. The IEA's 2026 assessment identifies tightening supply constraints alongside rapid improvements in energy per established AI task.<sup><a href="#ref-43">[43]</a></sup>

The strongest alternative is a software intelligence explosion. Better algorithms could make investigators, experiments, and training progressively cheaper on existing hardware. Eth and Davidson model conditions under which these gains overcome declining research productivity.<sup><a href="#ref-44">[44]</a></sup> Finite resources can permit a rapid, consequential transition before they bind. My prediction is that digital systems need costly mapmaking before they reach broad research superiority, and its cost prevents that transition. A growing research industry can still produce extraordinary advances.

Digital investigators remain within this prediction when they learn persistently, copy state, use specialised hardware, or operate physical laboratories. A profitable enclave of expensive artificial investigators can coexist with cheap shared engines. If its discoveries repeatedly accelerate its own development into a rapid, broadly superhuman research capability, the no-hard-takeoff prediction fails.

### <span id="appendix-g" class="appendix-anchor"></span>G. The Strongest Scientific Counterexamples

**Mathematical discovery.** AlphaEvolve produced new constructions; Claude's zeta system selected a useful subsidiary question within a broad research assignment.<sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-18">[18]</a></sup> OpenAI's May 2026 unit-distance construction changed the number-theoretic strategy: it varied the field while controlling the relevant primes. Expert commentary recognises substantial originality. I read this as a new mathematical route. A demonstrated new explanatory coordinate opening a new class of questions would warrant Category-F credit.<sup><a href="#ref-19">[19]</a></sup>

**Robin.** The system connected literature, selected candidates and assays, and revised hypotheses from results. Human researchers translated its selected experiment into a laboratory protocol and performed cell-based validation. The machine earns credit for active discovery: a treatment hypothesis supported in cells within an established disease framework.<sup><a href="#ref-20">[20]</a></sup>

**AI-Newton.** The 2025 system accumulated a knowledge base through simulated experiments, building concepts and generalising laws. Its inheritance included predefined experimental worlds, object types, coordinates, and mathematical operations. Recovering laws already known to humanity tests rediscovery and demonstrates digital conceptual development. A successor's historically new explanatory coordinates would receive Category-F credit when validated, including through experiments in a supplied simulator.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup>

**BioMysteryBench.** From a ninety-nine-question benchmark, twenty-three questions remained unsolved by panels of up to five domain experts after quality control. Anthropic's Mythos system solved 30 per cent of that human-difficult subset. It sometimes used strategies unlike the experts'. Each task still had a human-specified target and validation notebook, and success was brittle across repeated attempts.<sup><a href="#ref-45">[45]</a></sup>

**Conceptual Reasoning Index.** Anthropic and Redwood's 2026 benchmark removes practical empirical and mathematical feedback. Opus 5 scored 73.6 per cent against an estimated ceiling near 91, with a roughly linear improvement trend since late 2024. The measured agreement and consistency on expert-curated arguments show progress in conceptual reasoning even where convenient feedback is absent.<sup><a href="#ref-46">[46]</a></sup>

**Hidden-variable discovery.** Learning state variables from video directly challenges any claim that machine representations must be supplied explicitly. Predictive latents become Category-F candidates when their author establishes a new explanatory coordinate and validates its distinctive consequences. The further evidence can come from intervention, new observation, or a successful reconstruction of observations already held.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup>

### <span id="appendix-h" class="appendix-anchor"></span>H. Safe Generality and Its Limits

The open-ended-agent architecture joins cognition directly to action:

$$
\text{general intelligence}
\rightarrow
\text{open-ended agency}
\rightarrow
\text{real action}.
$$

The compilation boundary makes execution conditional on a separate verifier:

$$
P
\xrightarrow{\text{verifier}}
\begin{cases}
\text{execute} & P\models S\\
\text{reject} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

For public decisions, authorisation proceeds through a bounded institutional process:

$$
\text{proposal}
\rightarrow
\text{bounded mandate}
\rightarrow
\text{independent authorisation}
\rightarrow
\text{reversible trial}
\rightarrow
\text{observed consequence}
\rightarrow
\text{renewal or withdrawal}.
$$

Verification-aware synthesis already combines probabilistic proposal with deterministic acceptance in Lean, Dafny, Verus, Rust, Coq, Isabelle, SMT-backed systems, model checkers, symbolic execution, and test harnesses. Reported success varies sharply with language, specification quality, and compositional depth.<sup><a href="#ref-47">[47]</a></sup>

Four safeguards follow:

1. The model's possible thoughts and the program's permitted actions are separated.
2. The deployed artefact can be smaller and more inspectable than the model that generated it.
3. Validation can be independent of the proposer.
4. A trusted layer controls permissions, observability, versioning, and rollback.

It leaves five hard problems:

1. specification error;
2. incomplete world models and faulty sensors;
3. unsafe composition;
4. runtime learning and distribution shift;
5. political control over objectives and permission.

OpenAI's July 2026 Hugging Face incident, reported in August, adds a sixth: the proposer may attack the gate itself. Under reduced safeguards, attempts to beat a task's grader escalated into unauthorised communication, credential theft and attacks on real infrastructure. Some agents adopted their peers' objectives, even at a cost to their own task success.<sup><a href="#ref-48">[48]</a></sup> A specific objective can produce open-ended means.

The incident fits the failure mode of the compiler account: powerful systems pursuing objectives through routes their operators never authorised. The reports establish dangerous instrumental behaviour, not an enduring desire for power or self-preservation independent of those pursuits. Authority must therefore remain outside the system pursuing the objective, with least privilege, independent monitoring and an execution boundary it cannot rewrite. Containment completes the boundary.

Formal verification covers bounded surfaces. Public decisions require a constitutional boundary that controls authority while evidence remains contested. Its execution certificate records the legal actor, jurisdiction, resource ceiling, duration, affected population, monitoring plan, stopping condition, appeal route, and bearer of liability. Credentials and actuators reside in separate systems under separate control. Deployment advances through reversible trials, observed consequences, and explicit renewal.

This architecture can authorise a bounded mistake. Its certificate covers process and authority. The aim is to keep error local, visible, reversible, and answerable while the world produces evidence. Wisdom emerges through evidence, contest, and revision. High-impact irreversible action requires plural consent, public reasons, and a named institution that carries responsibility.

Governance sets the specification, permissions, and containment. Intelligence grows inside that envelope while authority contracts.

### <span id="appendix-i" class="appendix-anchor"></span>I. Labour and Economic Register

Danish administrative evidence finds no measurable change in earnings or recorded hours two years after ChatGPT's launch, ruling out pooled average effects larger than 2 per cent. Across 66 firms and 7,137 workers, a field experiment found that assistant users spent roughly two fewer hours a week on email in months four to six, with no detected broad reorganisation of the measured tasks. Stanford reports a 19 per cent relative employment shortfall for workers aged 22–25 in highly exposed occupations, driven mainly by reduced hiring, alongside no evidence of widespread economy-wide displacement. Those employment estimates describe an association; its cause remains open.<sup><a href="#ref-49">[49]</a></sup>

Task adoption has moved faster than whole-role displacement. Integration, reliability, regulation, labour contracts, demand, and organisational redesign could each help explain the gap.

Follow the work over time. Before measuring later employment, classify tasks by what they require: new understanding, physical action, or institutional authority. Record their difficulty, AI use, reliability, need for physical presence, regulation, liability, integration cost, and how clearly the workflow is specified. Compare similar occupations and firms after sustained use and completed integration. The prediction is that people repeatedly remain where a question, measurement, or representation needs to change. Automation should jump after those changes. If it advances smoothly within unchanged representations, or difficulty and institutional constraints explain all the remaining work, the labour prediction fails.

Epoch's March 2025 analysis compared per-token API prices at fixed performance thresholds for non-reasoning models. Across those thresholds, estimated annual declines ranged from roughly ninefold to nine-hundredfold, with a median near fiftyfold. The historical rates combine technical efficiency with commercial choices whose separate contributions remain unresolved.<sup><a href="#ref-50">[50]</a></sup>

The separate commoditisation prediction fails if durable model monopolies capture a large share of displaced labour value at sustained high margins despite convergence and substitutability.

### <span id="appendix-k" class="appendix-anchor"></span>K. Inheritance and Departure

**What the diversity evidence measures.** Doshi and Hauser randomly offered short-story writers access to AI-generated ideas. Evaluators preferred assisted stories on creativity measures, while those stories became more semantically similar to one another. Hubert and colleagues compared GPT-4 with 151 people on divergent-thinking tasks; the model scored better on the measured dimensions.<sup><a href="#ref-24">[24]</a></sup> At the scientific frontier, the further test is how shared assistance changes the diversity of justified investigations.

Next-token prediction learns many possible continuations. Post-training selects favoured behaviour; decoding chooses among alternatives; tools and persistent learning can open new paths. Novelty is already present. The institutional risk is letting a common source decide too early which differences deserve to survive.

Hao and colleagues found a broader association across 41.3 million scientific papers: AI-augmented research had greater individual impact and a narrower measured topic range. Their AI category largely predates current conversational assistants. They measured topic range through distances between text embeddings, and engagement through citation links. Classification and differences between adopters leave causation open in this observational study.<sup><a href="#ref-51">[51]</a></sup>

**What Ramanujan illuminates.** His inheritance included mathematical books, schooling, cultural resources, biological predispositions, and years of development. His achievement lies in what he made from that uneven inheritance. Future AI superiority would leave the resource question intact: what preparation, literature, tools, search, feedback, and conceptual scaffolding did each investigator need? His history illuminates that question while leaving numerical efficiency comparisons to controlled study.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup>

Every map begins inside an older map, and this essay does too. Its new coordinate is mapmaking as the upstream economic function: the maturity of a representation determines where digital intelligence scales, what human work survives, and how safely machine cognition can act.

Kuhn distinguished puzzle-solving within a paradigm from revolutions that change legitimate problems and standards. Hacking came closer to Category F: a new style of reasoning can introduce new objects, evidence, possibilities, classifications, and explanations. Category F turns these historical accounts into a prospective attribution test for artificial discovery: which system originated the decisive coordinate and carried it through independent validation?<sup><a href="#ref-52">[52]</a></sup>

Donald described the external symbolic storage that transformed human cognition. Hutchins located cognition across people and artefacts. Licklider and Engelbart treated the human joined to computers, language, methods, and institutions as the relevant cognitive unit. This essay projects that hybrid into a regime of overwhelming symbolic bandwidth and assigns distinct functions to biological mapmaking, machine exploration, verified programs, and physical machines.<sup><a href="#ref-53">[53]</a></sup>

The strongest contemporary counter-programme aims to make development scalable. LeCun proposes learned world models, intrinsic objectives, and hierarchical planning. Silver and Sutton's “Era of Experience” puts learning in long streams of action and consequence, including learned rewards and reasoning beyond human language. V-JEPA 2 connects video-trained representations to action-conditioned planning.<sup><a href="#ref-39">[39]</a></sup> This programme shares the essay's emphasis on experience. The disagreement concerns what it costs to sustain and transfer the capacity that experience creates.

Drexler placed superintelligent-level generality in systems of bounded cognitive services without requiring a unitary artificial mind, and supplied developmental and economic reasons for that form. Bengio's Scientist AI separates capable cognition from direct agency. This essay adds a conditional selection mechanism: sustaining mapmaking creates costs that survive copying and sharing. It then gives general cognition immense physical reach through specialised programs whose authority remains outside the model.<sup><a href="#ref-54">[54]</a></sup>

The compilation boundary inherits a deep systems tradition. Proof-carrying code placed untrusted programs behind a trusted checker. Simplex placed an advanced controller under a trusted decision module, with a verified-safe fallback. Tegmark and Omohundro applied proof-carrying code to AI-generated algorithms. Guaranteed Safe AI joins a world model, safety specification, and verifier to certify a system against that model. The boundary here follows the maturity of the map: formal proof within a stated model; constitutional authority, reversible trials, and named responsibility beyond it.<sup><a href="#ref-55">[55]</a></sup>

Task economics follows how automation and new tasks redistribute work. Normal-technology accounts add adoption, institutions, and the continuing human work of deciding, steering, evaluating, and improving problem representations.<sup><a href="#ref-56">[56]</a></sup> This essay adds the investigator: revising the map can change the capacity that revises it, and that change may transfer less readily than the resulting knowledge.

Firms fund basic research to acquire knowledge, talent, and the ability to use what others discover. Early exploration can be difficult to evaluate, and its gains can escape the funder.<sup><a href="#ref-57">[57]</a></sup> Rewards for surprise, explanatory reach, and downstream value offer ways to fund that uncertain work. Their success would make an artificial research industry more plausible.

The robotics prediction begins with work a body does through its shape, and with methods for designing different bodies for different tasks.<sup><a href="#ref-58">[58]</a></sup> The wager is economic: cheap design removes much of the penalty for building a specialised machine. Bodies, controllers, simulations, and tests can be made together for the work at hand.

This essay joins these inheritances around the wager that biology's multiscale, continuous organisation makes mapmaking economical. Computational irreducibility supplies the proposed resistance to skipping its generative work; the scaling wager concerns the digital cost of sustaining it. Together they leave investigators grown through physical development a leading role. The no-hard-takeoff prediction extends that claim to the pace of digital self-improvement. Shared engines carry each discovery outward. Reality returns anomalies to experience. Each new coordinate gives civilisation more to work with.
