# The soul of Age of Wonders

Preserve the wonder. Strive for simplicity, beauty, and humanity.

Age of Wonders begins in the distance between what the universe contains and what a person can reach.

Sunlight falls on a village whose sewing machine sits idle. A star bends light from a world we cannot yet see. A processor loses its market price while retaining the power to do useful work. These are material facts with human consequences. The writing brings the two close enough to touch.

Its deepest conviction is that the world offers more than we have learned to use, and that making it available to another person is worthy work. Wonder enlarges the possible. Care gives it a purpose.

This is the editorial standard for the essays in `src/content/essays/`. It replaces the former prose rules in `CLAUDE.md`. Its examples come from the canon. As the essays grow, the guide should grow with them.

## The standard

A reader should enter the sentence easily, feel what it opens, and trust the ground beneath it.

Simplicity removes unnecessary effort. Beauty gives the thought its living form. Humanity gives the scale someone to matter to. Wonder leaves the reader with more to imagine than the sentence can contain.

High signal includes a change in feeling, attention, or possibility. A sentence can make the world feel larger before the reader can explain why. Its meaning may keep unfolding after its words are understood.

The best passage usually does several things at once. A physical image explains a mechanism. A verb reveals a relationship. A small gesture carries a civilisational consequence. The words remain ordinary because the thing they disclose is extraordinary.

The governing movement is:

> Something is here. Something keeps it out of reach. Here is how that could change. Here is whose life would change with it.

This helps the writer find the argument. The reader may enter through a person, a measurement, a question, or the feeling of standing at an edge. The order belongs to the subject.

## Have a point of view

The canon takes a position. It chooses, predicts, believes. A reader should encounter a person willing to stand behind an idea and risk being wrong about it. That vulnerability is part of the beauty.

Protect the assertion at the heart of an essay. Give it clear words and enough room to make its case. An editor can strengthen the reasoning, find a better image, or correct a source while preserving the author's commitment. Disagreement is something the writing can invite.

Qualification should carry information. Keep an *if* that names the hinge of an argument. Keep *I expect* when the personal voice takes responsibility for a prediction. Cut the accumulating *perhaps*, *might*, *could*, and *on this account* when they merely soften a position the essay exists to advance. A conjecture can be identified once and then pursued with conviction.

There are two responsibilities here. The source must say what we claim it says. The author's interpretation must be recognisable as their own. A forecast or philosophical claim can reach beyond consensus and remain open to refutation. Its force comes from the reasons offered and the willingness to answer for them.

Beware the editor's representational gravity. A familiar framework can absorb a difficult thesis until the revision argues for something else. Reconstruct the author's mechanism before testing it. Strengthen or replace an unsupported premise openly; preserve a risky inference as a claim the author owns. Use the strongest rival to test that claim on its own terms. Check that the revised essay still asserts the same consequential idea.

Ask of the revision: can a reader still say plainly what this writer believes? Is there something left to disagree with? Preserve the risk that makes the answer yes.

## Leave room for the reader

Some vagueness is essential. *A wondrous future*, *the ground in everyone*, *what we choose to create*: these phrases leave room for a life the writer cannot specify on the reader's behalf. Their openness is part of their humanity.

Distinguish an open horizon from a missing explanation. A manifesto can begin by making a moment felt. A calculation needs its units. A metaphor can hold several meanings. A causal claim needs the connection between its parts. Edit each according to the work it is doing.

In the first pass on this canon, the opening of *Create an Age of Wonders* became a neat inventory of intelligence, materials, and life. It was more explicit and less beautiful. The original gives us a shared position—“We're standing at the edge”—then opens a future shaped by our choices. The technologies arrive in the sections that follow. Naming them sooner took something away.

Preserve that interval between invitation and explanation. Let a scene unfold before explaining its significance. Let a question remain open when the essay has brought the reader somewhere worth wondering about. Let an ending resonate beyond its last word.

### Show the constraint; leave the possibility alive

The constraint often has a physical form: a black hole evaporates, a processor wears out, drag takes part of a vehicle's speed. Put that fact on the page. The reader can feel its consequence.

The first pass added “This is a physical bound, not a machine we know how to build” to *Singularity*. The theoretical rate and the fleeting lifetime already carried the distinction. The extra sentence told the reader where to stop imagining. It has been removed. The rate stays; the lifetime is visible in the callout and the appendix.

Prefer the affirmative account. Show what exists, what moves, what costs, what changes. Where an assumption matters, give it a natural place: *in the model*, *suppose*, *the mission designs aim for*. Establish the frame, then let the passage live inside it. Renew the frame when the argument crosses into a different kind of claim.

Minimise negative constructions, especially the editorial reflex “not X, but Y”. Keep a negative when it carries an essential distinction or an irreplaceable cadence. “The scale will not answer” belongs to the meditation. “This does not establish it” appended to an already conditional thought usually belongs in the editor's notes.

The aim is a reader who can follow the argument and still see beyond it.

## Passages to learn from

Read the surrounding paragraphs as well as the quoted line. The unit of excellence is often the passage: what it establishes, what it leaves for the reader, and where it stops.

### A beginning opens a horizon

> These tools are opening the door to a wondrous future, shaped by what we choose to create.

[Create an Age of Wonders, “The Moment We're In”](src/content/essays/create-an-age-of-wonders.mdx).

The sentence offers neither a timetable nor a picture of the finished world. It offers participation. *Wondrous* leaves the future large; *we choose* brings it within human agency. Its familiar language is hospitable. A stranger can enter before learning the canon's vocabulary.

This is the manifesto's register. Its opening gathers people around a possibility. The essay then gives that possibility matter, mechanisms, and work. Preserve the welcome.

### A hand reaches before a civilisation sees

> He reached for his camera.

In [Perceptual Abundance, “Home”](src/content/essays/perceptual-abundance.mdx), Anders has just seen Earth above the lunar horizon. The sentence gives us an ordinary response to an unprecedented view. No adjective tells us to be moved. The hand, the camera, and the photograph do that work.

This is also a lesson in proportion. The paragraph before supplies the place and occasion. The image after supplies the scale. The sentence between them can be small. Expanding it to “He instinctively grasped the profound significance of the moment” would replace an act with the writer's interpretation of it.

Look for the gesture that carries the meaning before writing the meaning out.

### A number acquires a body

> Graphene aerogel, essentially frozen air with a carbon skeleton, has a density of 0.16 milligrams per cubic centimetre.
>
> You can balance it on a flower petal without bending the stem.

[Create an Age of Wonders, “Materials That Defy Intuition”](src/content/essays/create-an-age-of-wonders.mdx), with the intervening helium comparison omitted here.

The number establishes a property; the petal makes it imaginable. “Frozen air with a carbon skeleton” helps the reader picture the structure. The comparison performs explanatory work before it performs decorative work.

The passage stays with lightness long enough for us to feel it. Learn that patience: give a remarkable property something familiar to meet. The petal makes a number part of the reader's world.

### A short line inherits an explanation

> The magic is in the assembly.

In [Create an Age of Wonders, “Quantum Computing”](src/content/essays/create-an-age-of-wonders.mdx), this follows muscles, shells, spider silk, and coral made from familiar elements. “Assembly” names what those examples share. “Magic” belongs because the preceding matter has earned the feeling.

The line is valuable for what it compresses. Writing a seven-word sentence elsewhere will not reproduce the effect. Without the examples, it becomes a slogan. With three more explanations after it, it loses its air.

Earn the short sentence. Do not write towards a word count.

### The benefit is felt at the end of the line

> Sunlight lands pre-distributed, on roofs, fields, and mountainsides. The last village pays no distance premium.

[It's All Energy, “Delivery”](src/content/essays/its-all-energy.mdx).

The first sentence names the mechanism and gives it places. The second translates it into the experience of someone normally expensive to reach. “Last” carries the history of exclusion without requiring an argument about justice in the same breath.

Its scope matters: sunlight has arrived, but a working electricity system still needs panels, storage, maintenance, and finance. The surrounding essay must keep that distinction clear. The line should remain graceful because the explanation around it remains honest.

### The apparent ruin contains the next use

> Hardware depreciates. Algorithms compound.

[Computational Abundance, “Stockfish for Everything”](src/content/essays/computational-abundance.mdx).

The same piece of silicon is the subject of two different clocks. Parallel grammar makes their opposing directions immediately visible. This is a contrast worth retaining: neither half contains the argument alone.

The preceding account of smaller models and better software matters more than the symmetry. When a future editor meets another attractive pair of sentences, the question is whether there are two real processes underneath it. Antithesis is useful when the distinction is the information.

### A machine enters the dark

> In compute terms, one pod is a modern AI supercomputer: the kind a government announces as a national asset. Leviathan lowers it into the dark.

[Leviathan, “The Unit”](src/content/essays/leviathan.mdx).

The comparison makes the pod's computational scale familiar. The next sentence supplies weight, depth, and movement. A national asset becomes one part of a larger machine. The verb *lowers* accomplishes more than another paragraph calling the project immense.

The essay must first establish that Leviathan is a proposal. Once the scene has that frame, its present tense can remain vivid. Repeating “hypothetically” in every line would make the reading worse without improving its honesty.

### Engineering can supply its own metaphor

> The mountain carries what the vehicle never could.

[Bridge to Infinity, “The Workhorse Stage”](src/content/essays/bridge-to-infinity.mdx).

The mountain carries infrastructure; that infrastructure takes work off the vehicle. The ordinary verb describes both the structure and the argument. The metaphor is already inside the design.

The appendix gives another version: a first stage “made of bedrock and electricity instead of aluminium and kerosene”. The materials explain the proposed change. This is more powerful than calling the launcher revolutionary because it lets the reader see what the revolution would consist of.

### Two halves name one instrument

> The physics is the instrument. Computation is the camera.

[The Solar Gravitational Lens, “From Pixels to Geography”](src/content/essays/the-solar-gravitational-lens.mdx).

The Sun bends light; a spacecraft samples it; reconstruction turns the samples into a map. After that explanation, the pair gives the reader two handles for a difficult idea. Its balance comes from the division of work, not from a wish to sound balanced.

The explanation gives the reader enough to imagine the work. The image then has room to become geography: coastlines, clouds, the possibility of a place. Carry the method clearly and let the view open.

### Freedom has a physical form

> It carries its own harbour.

[The Free Starship, “What Remains”](src/content/essays/the-free-starship.mdx).

The essay has spent its pages explaining fuel, repair, life support, and long journeys. The harbour gathers those provisions into one object the reader already understands. It also carries shelter and the possibility of leaving.

The sentence can be figurative because the account of autonomy is concrete. It would be weaker as “It possesses autonomous logistical capabilities.” It would be dishonest if used to imply that the vessel had solved every dependency. The remaining silicon clock makes the freedom more interesting.

### The record begins in somebody's hands

> The filings settled into curves. He warmed the wax and fixed them to the page.

[Symbolic Superintelligence, “Change the Coordinates”](src/content/essays/symbolic-superintelligence.mdx).

Faraday's hands give the essay's mapmaking argument an origin. The reader can follow an invisible phenomenon becoming a durable mark. The verbs occur in the order of the work: settled, warmed, fixed.

This passage is clearer than the later vocabulary of ontologies and epistemic transduction because it shows what those terms are trying to name. Preserve the technical distinction when it becomes necessary. Begin by making the act intelligible.

### Experience changes its keeper

> The past changes the brain that remembers it.

[Irreducible, “Twenty Watts”](src/content/essays/irreducible.mdx). This essay remains available as a superseded source; Symbolic Superintelligence occupies its former place in the sequence.

The sentence follows a physical account of memory and plasticity. It makes that account personal without adding a new mechanism. “The past” belongs to a life; “the brain” belongs to biology; “changes” joins them.

Its editorial lesson survives the essay's supersession: an account of biology can become an account of a life through one exact verb. Preserve that passage between the physical and the personal.

### The final verb gives the belief a task

> Take what is abundant, and carry it to those it has not yet reached.

[Love is the Foundation, closing line](src/content/essays/love-is-the-foundation.mdx).

The verbs are *take* and *carry*. The object is abundance; the destination is a person. The sentence contains the canon's ethics without naming an ethical system. Its length is right for its movement. A rule demanding a closer of fewer than eight words would destroy it.

Care, teaching, engineering, and sharing knowledge can all fulfil the instruction. Agency is returned without pretending that everyone should build the same thing.

### The largest scale ends in an ordinary act

> Love is why you walk it.
>
> If you can ask whether the universe is with you, you are already the answer.
>
> So keep walking.

[Singularity, “The Smallest Thing”](src/content/essays/singularity.mdx).

The essay turns from speculative cosmology to a reason to live. Its final verb is deliberately ordinary. After black holes and possible universes, walking restores a human scale.

The invitation can reach a reader who imagines the cosmos differently. The earlier sections carry the conjecture; the ending returns us to a life. Walking leaves each person free to picture their own next step.

## What these passages share

### Let the thought find a body

Often, introduce the thing before the name that organises it. A chip waits for a power connection; then the essay can name the *slot*. Faraday preserves the filings; then the essay can name *mapmaking*. The Sun bends light; then the essay can explain a gravitational lens.

An abstraction may also open the way: “Humanity has made a map.” Give it a body as the passage unfolds. The opening can promise the encounter before it delivers it.

Words such as *abundance*, *access*, *frontier*, *substrate*, *coherence*, and *infrastructure* are useful only while their referents remain clear. If three occur in one sentence, try writing what the people or machines are doing. The problem is usually not that the reader lacks vocabulary. The writer has compressed the relationship before explaining it.

Use a technical word when it preserves a distinction that ordinary language would lose. Define it where the reader first needs it. Do not replace *bandwidth* with *speed* when the distinction from latency is the argument.

### A paragraph makes a movement

One sentence may establish a fact; the next may explain it; the third may show the consequence. They belong together if they carry one movement. Splitting every sentence into its own paragraph makes all three sound equally momentous.

Let paragraphs differ in length. An explanation needs room. A moment of recognition may need a line. Give the reader enough continuity to follow the reasoning and enough space to feel its changes.

A paragraph is too dense when the reader must remember several unexplained terms or reverse direction to find the subject. Word count can locate candidates for inspection. It cannot decide the edit.

### Disclose depth gradually

The main body carries one continuous argument. Give the reader the thing, then the reason it matters, then the detail they need to go further. A catalogue of evidence can obscure a clear idea as thoroughly as jargon can.

Give each section a new job. Once a mechanism is understood, let its consequence carry the reader forward. Reintroducing the thesis under each heading makes a long essay feel stationary. Distinguish repeated explanation from a refrain: a visual element can repeat a thought to give it weight, let the reader breathe, or make its scale felt.

Short sentences do not require separate paragraphs. Keep premise, explanation, and consequence together when they make one movement. Save isolation for a genuine arrival. Joining fragments alone cannot cure an overgrown body: cut repeated work and relocate secondary detail before repairing the rhythm.

Use appendices as a second depth of reading. Deal terms, quarterly results, price comparisons, alternative cases, and derivations belong where a curious reader can examine them without interrupting the argument. Link to the question the detail answers. Keep essential conditions beside the claim they govern.

Give an opinionated essay one clear throughline. The body carries the examples, mechanism, and consequence that earn its wager. The appendices carry detailed comparisons, competing explanations, uncertainties, and tests that could overturn it. Moving analysis deeper should make the claim easier to follow and its grounds easier to inspect. Keep the author's leap visible where it begins; the reader then has a position to consider, rather than a debate to moderate.

*Computational Abundance* illustrates the distinction. The body follows a chip from its missing power connection to the work it can do in someone's hands. The appendices hold the financing, project inventory, rental prices, and crossover model. The reader can finish the essay without opening them, then return with a question.

Read the main body straight through, with every appendix closed. It should feel complete at that depth. Then read it with its visual pauses: some thoughts deserve to be encountered twice, once understood and once felt. Read the deeper material separately: it should reward curiosity with explanation. Length is earned by movement, not by the amount of research available.

### Verbs carry relationships

Prefer a person or thing doing something: a current carries heat away; a test rejects a program; a child reaches for what surprised it. These constructions are simple because the relationship is visible.

Nouns can conceal that relationship. “The optimisation of deployment under constraint” leaves the reader to discover who acts, what changes, and what resists. “The operator puts newer chips in the scarce powered rack” supplies all three.

Passive voice is useful when the affected thing belongs at the centre: *The crew is protected by the tanks around it.* The test is attention, not grammatical purity.

### Music comes from variation

Longer sentences can gather a catalogue, follow a process, or sustain a view. Short sentences can mark the turn. Neither should become the default rhythm of every paragraph.

Keep contractions when they make the voice natural. Keep a fragment when its missing words are already present in the reader's mind. Keep an *and* when it carries accumulation. A semicolon can clarify a genuine parallel; a full stop can release a finished thought.

Read several paragraphs together. If every section opens with a maxim, supplies three examples, and closes with two opposed fragments, the method has become audible. Restore ordinary explanatory sentences. Allow a section to end quietly when the next one needs the momentum.

### An image can explain or open

Many of the strongest images are local to the subject: a lens, a harbour, a brick, a map, a current, a hand. Some make a mechanism visible. Others give us a feeling, a relation, or a horizon. “The universe is in us” reaches beyond a single explanation; that is part of its work.

Keep one governing image through a passage. A computer need not become an organism, a city, a forge, and a cathedral in the same paragraph. Each image asks the reader to rebuild the scene.

Distinguish evocative language from evidence. “Frozen air” helps us imagine an aerogel. “A simulated storm does not get wet” introduces a philosophical distinction. Neither proves an unrelated material or consciousness claim. State the next premise if the argument needs one.

### Give conviction a clear shape

A conviction deserves a direct sentence. A reported result deserves an accurate account. Know which one you are editing.

*Under these assumptions*, *in this study*, and *for this workload* can carry essential information about a calculation or observation. Place that information where it helps the reader understand the result. Let the essay's larger argument speak in its own voice.

For a technical explanation, “Tests catch many coding errors quickly” gives a clear account of what happens. For a thesis, “Digital intelligence will do most of the work inside the map” states a view the following pages undertake to defend. Both sentences are direct. They carry different responsibilities.

Know what kind of claim carries the argument. Give the reader the context needed to follow it:

| Kind | What the reader needs |
|---|---|
| Observation | What happened, when, and how it was measured. |
| Calculation | Inputs, units, assumptions, and the scope of the result. |
| Inference | The reasoning connecting evidence to conclusion. |
| Proposal | What exists already and what still needs to work. |
| Prediction | What the author expects and why; a date when timing matters to the argument. |
| Speculation | Where the leap begins and what makes it worth following. |
| Value or faith | A conviction the writer owns, and the life or work it asks of us. |

These are questions for the writer, not labels to stamp on every paragraph. The sentence can carry its scope lightly. “Suppose a black hole could give birth to a universe” opens the speculative passage; within that frame, the writer can explore the world it suggests.

Let equations show their assumptions and sources show what was observed. Keep the essential condition visible in a caption that can travel alone. Prefer that concrete information to a general warning about the limits of knowledge.

### The warmth is in the attention

Do not turn people into illustrations of a thesis. The mother wants to sell music and cold drinks. That particular wish matters more than calling her an example of productive energy demand.

Keep an individual's choices, skills, and aims visible. People beyond the grid already make lives; electricity changes what those lives can hold. The essay should recognise their agency before inviting the builder's.

Never invent reporting to create contact. An observed scene needs a source or the author's confirmed account. A hypothetical scene must be recognisable as imagined. A quotation must be checked or marked as uncertain before its apparent authority does any work.

### Wonder ends in a change of understanding

A section does not always need an aphorism. It needs to leave the reader somewhere the section has earned: able to picture the instrument, follow the constraint, recognise the implication, or ask the next necessary question.

The essay's final line can return to its opening object, name the consequence, offer a task, or rest in an image. It need not summarise the whole argument. “So keep walking” works because of what *it* refers to; “Courage is” needs the preceding sentence.

A strong ending is often the place to remove a sentence. Cut the explanation after the arrival. An essay earns its ending through what it makes us see as well as what it demonstrates. The final assertion may carry the writer's largest conviction. Let it stand exposed.

## Distinct essays, one standard

The current reading order is defined in the site's `CANONICAL_ORDER` arrays. As reviewed in September 2026, it contains eleven essays. `Irreducible` is a published, superseded predecessor, not a twelfth slot. Preserve that distinction and the ordering.

The links should follow the argument. The lens supplies prospective destinations for the starship; the starship supplies a possible relay back to the lens. Energy supplies the watts for computation and Leviathan. Leviathan proposes a source for the models Computational Abundance distributes. Symbolic Superintelligence asks what people originate and what machines can safely carry out. Love supplies the purpose; Singularity tests a wider interpretation.

Link a prior essay where its idea is doing work. A reader arriving directly should still understand the paragraph. Near the end of the collection, a gathering of earlier images can become a homecoming: the watt, the lens, the ship, brought back to the life they serve. Preserve that cumulative music when it makes the whole newly felt.

Manifesto, discovery, analysis, engineering proposal, and philosophical essay need different balances. The manifesto can declare a conviction early. Analysis must let the reader follow a mechanism patiently. An engineering proposal can stage a future once it identifies that future as a proposal. A philosophical essay must give a serious rival account enough room to remain recognisable. None benefits from imitating another's surface rhythm.

## Every place the reader touches

### Title and description

The title gives the essay an identity. The description gives a stranger a reason to enter. It should add something, not praise the essay as profound, bold, or transformative.

Read title, description, opening, and final line together. They should belong to the same argument without saying the same sentence four times. Metadata is a claim too. A description that turns a proposal into an accomplished fact needs revision even if it fits elegantly on a card.

Changing a title or frontmatter description also requires updating the generated OG image through `scripts/generate-og-images.mjs`. Check the site's cards, document metadata, feed, and share copy for agreement.

### Headings

The headings tell the story in miniature. A reader who skips the paragraphs should still grasp most of the argument: what holds us back, what changes it, and what opens beyond. Read the sequence on its own. Each heading should move the thought forward.

The best combine meaning with wonder. “Where the Sun Lives” makes the geography of energy human. “Turning the Sun into a Telescope” names the extraordinary mechanism. “Cataloguing the Galaxy” gives that mechanism a purpose. “Representational Gravity” makes an intellectual force memorable. In *Love is the Foundation*, “The Market Stands on What It Cannot Produce” carries an argument before the first paragraph begins.

Name the discovery or consequence, not just its filing category. “The Geometry”, “The Power”, and “The Mechanism” ask the paragraph to do all the work. “The Sea Gives Another Dimension” lets the reader see what the geometry changes. “The Desert Powers the Deep” joins source and destination. The magic comes from the actual relationship, not an ornamental adjective.

Vary the cadence. A direct claim, an image, a question, and a short name can belong to the same sequence. “A Delivery” leaves a revelation for the paragraph; “Where the Fire Burns” gives a technical constraint a physical presence. The sequence carries the argument. Each heading need not explain itself completely. Preserve anticipation, human scale, and room for discovery alongside the clear claims.

The sequence also has an emotional direction. In *Singularity*, discovery opens doors, computation reaches black holes, black holes give birth to universes, and the scale expands to “A Universe Chooses Humanity”. “For The Smallest Thing” then brings the grandeur back to care. The return gives the expansion its purpose. In *Symbolic Superintelligence*, “Drawing a World” and “We Are Wanderers” let a reader feel the distinction between a map and the people who make it. Prefer the heading that brings an idea into human experience when the paragraph already supplies its technical account.

Let events carry the sequence. *Leviathan* begins when a computer outgrows the grid. A supercomputer is lowered into the dark; the sea gives another dimension; the desert powers the deep. A “Tesseract of Supercomputers” waits for light and becomes a forge. The last heading returns to where land meets sea. These are turns in a story, each changing what the reader can imagine next. Read the headings aloud as a continuous passage. Listen for movement, scale, suspense and return.

Small connecting words can carry the journey between sections. “From Worlds of Fuel” continues the ship's passage; “For a City Between Two Worlds” gives the preceding engineering a destination. “The Ship Becomes a Vessel” makes maintenance part of a transformation. “The Earth Breathes” lets the consequence happen. “Building Our Cathedral” makes a distant programme a shared undertaking, and “Our Pale Blue Dot” brings the voyage home. Let headings lean towards one another. They need not be self-contained summaries when together they make a living sentence.

Let the sentence establish its own register. “Suppose a black hole could give birth to a universe” opens a conjecture clearly. A separate announcement that speculation follows interrupts the entrance. Preserve the exact condition that makes a claim honest; remove the editorial preamble that merely announces what the next sentence does.

This standard covers the canon and both dossiers. In a dossier, bring the reader towards the machine, the measurement, the decision, or the life it could change. Keep precise gate names, numbered cross-references, evidence registers, and technical lookup labels where they help someone find or test a claim. A heading must preserve the distinction between a measured result, a proposal, and a possibility.

Test the headings in the rendered page and navigation, at desktop and phone widths. Keep their hierarchy and existing link targets intact. A more beautiful name should make the document easier to follow and easier to find your way back through.

### Figures, captions, and alt text

A figure should show something that matters to the argument. The caption identifies what we are looking at and what to notice. Label concepts, simulations, and artist's impressions where they appear; the reader may reach the image before its surrounding explanation.

Alt text describes the visual for someone who cannot see it. It is neither a second sales pitch nor a place for hidden claims. Credit and source links belong with the figure.

The scientific distinction travels with the image. A simulated reconstruction is not an observed exoplanet. The date an image was released may differ from the date its light was collected.

### Pull quotes and components

Visual elements shape the pace as well as the argument. A pull quote can let a line resonate. A data insight can turn a large number into something the reader can hold. Repetition is welcome when it creates a pause, emphasis, or recognition; cut it when it merely explains again. Read the surrounding passage to judge the difference.

Choose what deserves the reader's attention. “A gigawatt of computing in the span of a large building” reveals Leviathan's scale. “In the plume model, a compact hundred-gigawatt field exceeds the assumed thermal limit” elevates a technical qualification into the emotional centre of the page. That detail belongs in the explanation. The visual pause belongs to the idea the reader should carry away.

`DataInsight` needs a claim and enough context to interpret it: unit, comparison, date, or condition. `PullQuote` can hold the earned line. `Callout` can organise a definition or a genuinely parallel set. `SummaryBox` belongs only where a long argument needs a compact restatement; it is not an automatic coda.

Imagine the component detached from the essay. Give it enough context to remain true, in the lightest language that does the job. A proposed machine can be named as a proposal; a theoretical rate can carry its fleeting lifetime. Keep the striking idea in the foreground. If accuracy needs a paragraph of conditions, choose another moment for emphasis.

### Appendices and references

The body carries the argument and the author's commitment to it. The appendix gives readers the detail needed to examine its foundations. Keep a condition in the body when the reader needs it to understand a result; give fuller assumptions, rival explanations, and tests room in the appendix. The essay can move with conviction while making its reasoning available for scrutiny.

The appendix keeps the same voice at greater depth. Begin with the question a reader has brought from the essay. Explain what the calculation, experiment, or comparison lets them see. Introduce technical terms beside the work they do; give dense comparisons a table when that makes them easier to follow. Each paragraph should make one intelligible movement. Precision belongs in clear nouns, units, and conditions. A change of depth should preserve the welcome.

Begin a calculation with its question. Define symbols and units before using them. Label original modelling. Explain what the result means and what it does not settle. A memorable opening line is welcome if it helps; verification prose need not compete with the essay's ending.

Give each quantity its boundary once, where it enters the argument: optical input or electricity delivered, emitting surface or panel area, an average or a peak. Carry that meaning through every table and comparison. A precise noun often does the work of several qualifications. Keep a model's expectation distinct from a physical ceiling, and a chosen design margin distinct from a law of nature. Let the appendix earn the body's freedom.

Use the strongest fair comparison. Compare subsea cooling with modern land immersion as well as a conventional facility. Separate light-transit delay from the time a whole network exchange takes. Separate the cost of launching material from the cost of extracting and processing it.

References should name the actual work, date, and location supporting the nearby claim. A publisher's homepage or a generic phrase such as “project appraisal documents” is a lead to follow, not a finished citation. Keep quotation, paraphrase, and the author's modelling distinguishable.

## Revising towards the standard

### First, find what must survive

Read the essay through without editing. State its argument in one ordinary sentence. Mark the passages that supply its voice: the gesture, example, difficult distinction, or ending that another writer would be unlikely to have found.

Write a short keep list before a change list. Include invitations, silences, repetitions, and open-ended language as well as exact images. Revision should know what it is protecting. “He reached for his camera” does not need improvement because a new editor has arrived.

Then describe what each section changes in the reader's understanding. If two sections make the same move, combine or cut. If a conclusion requires a premise the reader never received, supply the premise before polishing the conclusion.

### Make the causal chain readable

For each major passage, locate the resource, constraint, mechanism, consequence, and unresolved condition. The essay may not need all five in every section. The editor needs to know where they are.

Translate a dense paragraph into spoken explanation. Restore the precise terms only where they earn their space. Check that the translation preserves the original distinction. Split at changes of work, not mechanically at conjunctions.

### Keep the better sentence, not the stricter sentence

Actual revisions from this review illustrate different kinds of improvement:

| Earlier text | Revision | Editorial reason |
|---|---|---|
| “The grid stopped on a curve.” | “The further a grid reaches into sparsely settled country, the harder each connection is to pay for.” | A longer sentence can ask less of the reader. |
| “Performs a long-duration skim” | “Skims for fuel” | The verb and purpose replace operational paperwork. |
| “This was the first time we saw Earth as a whole.” | “We had gone to the Moon and found ourselves looking back.” | The human reversal preserves wonder without a false historical first. |
| “A scalar continuum leaves the harder tail of already constituted tasks.” | “If capability alone governs the boundary, the hardest existing tasks should be the last to fall.” | The condition and prediction become legible. |
| “The limit was only ever twenty kilometres.” | “A way off Earth, built into it.” | The close returns to the mountain without claiming that altitude solves every constraint. |

These are examples of decisions, not substitutions to repeat. A later revision may find a better line still.

### Read again for what the edit took away

Compare the revised passage with the original for welcome, music, human presence, conviction, and room to imagine. A shorter sentence may have lost its breath. A more explicit sentence may have closed its horizon. A more qualified sentence may have lost the reason it was written. Repetition may have been carrying the reader somewhere.

The second pass restored “Night is becoming a manufacturing problem” in place of a sentence about storage costs. It restored the meditation beginning “The scale will not answer” instead of summarising its conclusion. It restored the thread of light carrying data home in *The Solar Gravitational Lens*. Each restoration recovered an experience the literal account had lost.

For constraints, try showing the thing itself. “Drag still takes its share” tells us more than “the full trajectory still needs validation”. A message that still has its recipient shows what remains after a software rollback. This is precision a reader can inhabit.

Ask: what becomes possible in the reader's mind after this passage? Leave them that space.

### Read for breath and trust

Read aloud, or subvocalise carefully if no spoken read is available. Do not report an audible read that did not happen. Mark stumbles, repeated beats, and places where the reader must hold too much before reaching a verb.

Then read as a sceptical specialist. Check the measurements, sources, and reasoning. Locate the point where evidence becomes the author's judgement, and make that move intelligible. A reader should be able to challenge the view at its strongest.

Read once more for conviction. Look for qualifications added chiefly to protect the writer from disagreement. Remove them. Keep the ones that tell the reader something necessary.

Finally, read as someone new to the canon. They should not need a technical education to parse the syntax, or prior agreement with the worldview to follow the argument.

## Rules that must remain subordinate to judgement

The former guide identified five recurring devices: the compression drop, physical anchor, staircase close, threshold sentence, and access inversion. All can work. None is compulsory.

Do not add a missing device to make an essay sound canonical. Diagnose the missing function: a concrete referent, a causal link, a fair qualification, breathing room, or an ending. Sometimes the repair is one more explanatory sentence. Sometimes it is deletion.

Retire these mechanical rules:

- A good close must be under eight words. The close of *Love is the Foundation* disproves it.
- Closing sentences must get progressively shorter. Meaning should concentrate; lengths can vary.
- Every section must land an aphorism. This makes a long essay exhausting.
- A forecast can borrow the inevitability of a physical law. Trends require conditions and horizons.
- All hedges, intensifiers, or compound sentences should be purged. Some carry scope, voice, or rhythm.
- Every numerical claim must leave the body. A necessary, dated number can orient the reader; a quarterly ledger usually belongs in the appendix.
- The appendix is where the essay tries to disprove itself and fails. It must be allowed to find a failure and change the essay.
- Moral claims are proved by physics. Physics describes capacities and constraints. The obligation to another person is a commitment the writing must own.

“Engineering scripture” can describe the union of material detail and moral seriousness. It can be fervent, exposed, and answerable. The reader meets a mind that has looked closely and chosen what it believes.

## House conventions and handoff

Use British spelling: *metre* for distance, *meter* for an instrument; *organisation*, *optimisation*, *aluminium*, *defence*, *datacentre*. Preserve proper names, source titles, and exact quotations. Keep units consistent within a comparison.

Set surviving em dashes closed: `word—word`. Preserve deliberate concept capitals when the essay has made the word a name. Use bold for the essay's final sentence and for genuine structural navigation, including the perceptual essay's recurring spine and named design reveals. Do not use it to make an ordinary claim seem stronger.

Before handing off a revision:

1. The writer's point of view is unmistakable, and each section advances it. The revision preserves its willingness to be wrong.
2. The keep list survived unless a specific problem justified a change.
3. Evidence, model, proposal, prediction, and belief remain distinguishable.
4. Numbers have units and fair comparisons; quotations and added factual claims have sources.
5. Captions, components, descriptions, and share text make claims the body can support.
6. The prose has variation, contact, and enough explanation to be read once.
7. The revision preserves wonder, human presence, and room for the reader. The ending has room to resonate.
8. Frontmatter, supersession, links, anchors, canonical order, and mathematical rendering remain intact.
9. The local build passes. Inspect representative routes and any changed visual surfaces at desktop and mobile widths.
10. Report what was changed, what was verified, and any remaining evidential limits. Publication remains a separate authorised action.

The guide succeeds when the reader encounters a clearer world, a living voice, and a larger sense of what might be possible.
