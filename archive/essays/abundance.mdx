A person can own a hundred bedrooms. They still sleep in one.

They can keep aircraft on three continents. They still take one journey at a time.

Every fortune meets the same constraint: one body, one place, twenty-four hours.

Wealth has no obvious ceiling. A life does.

Desire can keep climbing. Position stays scarce by definition. A body and a day still bound the reproducible services of a rich life: shelter, food, mobility, education, medicine and time.

Abundance gives everyone access to those services.

## I. One Body

At ordinary incomes, another dollar becomes a warmer room, a better meal, a safer car, a day free from work or a doctor seen in time.

At extreme wealth, another dollar is less likely to change the texture of a typical day. It enters a portfolio. It buys security, position, influence, companies or projects larger than one person.

Buying a billion-dollar company converts cash into ownership: a claim on future production and a voice in what will exist.

A fortune can always find another use. It can fund another laboratory, political campaign, telescope or railway. Those budgets may be open-ended. Their director still receives twenty-four hours.

Household and lottery studies show the same bend: higher-income households spend less of a wealth gain, and more-liquid households spend less of a windfall.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup>

The evidence shows a bend. That is enough. Money can keep buying security, choice and power after another material service barely changes the day.

Call that generous outer envelope the fifty-million-dollar life: the reproducible services extreme wealth buys at today's prices. The number gives the boundary a scale. <a class="xref" href="#appendix-c">Appendix C</a> treats it as an envelope.

  insight="The services of extreme wealth are easier to reproduce than extreme wealth itself."
  context="Reproducible comfort can spread. Rank, control and rarity remain scarce."
/>

In 1930, John Maynard Keynes called this the distinction between absolute and relative wants. Shelter can become sufficient. Relative rank keeps moving.<sup><a href="#ref-26">[26]</a></sup>

Fred Hirsch later separated the expandable material economy from the positional economy limited by nature, rank or congestion.<sup><a href="#ref-29">[29]</a></sup>

They asked the right question. The new variable is the supply machine.

We can manufacture watts. Copy intelligence. Automate work.

## II. Abundance Machinery

Take a home.

Clean power runs the quarry, crusher, kiln and furnace. Models design the structure for its site. Factories turn cement, glass, timber and steel into repeatable wall, roof, kitchen and bathroom modules. Robots move the heavy pieces, fasten them in place and inspect every joint. Sensors find faults before they become failures. Each completed home improves the design of the next.

The home still needs matter, capital, land, permission and maintenance. Automation concentrates its repeatable stages into reusable design and machinery. The next home inherits what the last one taught.

Energy supplied the capacity. Intelligence supplied the design. Automation supplied the hands.

Repeatable work can move onto a manufactured-cost curve (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-g">Appendix G</a>).

Every new site still needs its own kiln. A working model can be copied into machines at every kiln, quarry and construction site.

Now enlarge the productive budget.

Take one megawatt of productive capacity per person as the horizon for the industrial system behind each life. One megawatt is roughly four hundred times today's average primary-energy use per person. Across eight billion people it would equal about 6.6 per cent of the solar energy Earth already absorbs (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-d">Appendix D</a>, <a class="xref" href="#appendix-e">E</a>).

<StatCard
  number="1 MW"
  label="continuous productive capacity per person"
/>

A body metabolises about one hundred watts. One megawatt places ten thousand bodies' worth of productive power behind each actual body. That power lives in mines, farms, factories, grids, computers, transportation, aviation, and space.

At that scale, seawater becomes fresh water, lower-grade ores become usable metals and synthetic fuels carry clean power into ships and aircraft.

Energy becomes an industrial reagent: the capacity to separate, heat, cool, compress, refine, recycle and move matter whenever the result is worth more than the transformation.

Electricity purifies silicon and runs the line. Machine vision finds defects. Models improve yield. Robots move wafers, glass and frames. Better panels leave the factory and produce more electricity, which powers more factories.

<DataInsight
  insight="Energy builds machines. Machines build energy. Intelligence improves both."
  context="Each turn expands the productive system that powers the next."
/>

A cell takes simple feedstocks and assembles proteins, membranes, sensors, motors and copies of itself. It works in water, near room temperature, with molecular precision.

We have used microorganisms to make bread and alcohol for millennia. We now use engineered cells to make insulin, enzymes, ingredients and materials.<sup><a href="#ref-18">[18]</a></sup>

Computation is reaching the design layer. AlphaFold 3 predicts the joint structures of biomolecular complexes. CRISPR made targeted DNA editing far easier to program. In 2023, the FDA approved the first CRISPR/Cas9 treatment. Clinicians edit a patient's own blood-forming stem cells outside the body and return them.<sup><a href="#ref-16">[16]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup>

Samples become data. Models rank targets. Robotic laboratories test interventions. Production lines scale what survives validation (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-h">Appendix H</a>).

Clinicians can now attack some inherited diseases at the sequence itself. Researchers increasingly read cancer as thousands of measurable, evolving molecular problems. Medicine can train the immune system to recognise infections earlier. Tissue engineering is learning to repair, replace and regrow failing tissues.

Ageing is the deepest version of the same programme. Interacting failures accumulate across cells, organs and systems. Measure them earlier. Intervene more precisely. Learn from every outcome.

## III. Accessing Production

A warehouse can be full while a family goes hungry.

Amartya Sen showed that famine can occur even when food is available. The failure is entitlement: whether work, trade, ownership or public provision lets a person command it.<sup><a href="#ref-30">[30]</a></sup>

People experience production as abundance when they can command it.

For most households in industrial economies, labour income has been the main claim on production. Automation can break that link: output can expand while the wage share contracts.

The wage has been the bridge from production to distribution: contribute work, receive a claim on output. A machine can inherit the work. Institutions must preserve the claim.

A high-automation economy needs a durable universal claim on real output (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-i">Appendix I</a>).

Part comes through lower prices. Part through universal infrastructure: power, healthcare, education, mobility and connectivity. Part through ownership of productive capital.

New money chases rent when supply stays fixed. New output does not guarantee broad access if its ownership stays concentrated. Abundance requires productive capacity and broad command.

Markets and unequal fortunes can continue above a material floor that rises with the productive frontier.

If machines inherit more of the work, everyone must inherit a share of the machines.

The promise is freedom from material fear.

## IV. Some Scarcity is Irreducible

Excellent shelter can become universal. A particular harbour view remains singular.

Excellent care can become universal. One surgeon still has one hour to give.

An image can be copied without limit. The original remains one object.

Material production can expand. Position remains scarce. Institutions decide who receives what remains scarce and who writes the rules.

Scarcity changes address. It concentrates in singular places, finite attention, ecological sinks and authority over shared systems.

Cheap energy can desalinate water. It can also accelerate extraction. Better efficiency can reduce material per product while civilisation makes more products.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup>

Ecology sets the outer boundary.

Here, abundance means the reproducible material basis of a good life, within ecological limits, available to everyone.

Abundance delivers enough service while leaving room for the living world.

Manufacture can answer material shortage. Governance must answer position and power.

## V. Abundance is Ordinary

A child wakes in a beautiful home that one lost job cannot take away. Power is continuous. Water is clean. The ventilation filters smoke. The building stays cool through a heatwave and produces more energy than it needs.

A health system notices a change before it becomes a catastrophe. When illness appears, clinicians design treatment for this body.

A machine explains the lesson a hundred ways, in any language, with endless patience. A human teacher knows the child, the group and the place where they belong.

Their parent works because the work matters. Machines carry the dangerous weight and repetitive administration.

Machines may eventually build much of the world better than we can. That does not decide what the world is for. Those who live in it must retain the power to authorise, contest and revise its ends (<a class="xref" href="#appendix-j">Appendix J</a>).

Freedom lets a person leave a cruel employer, care for a parent, raise a child, learn a craft or attempt work whose return is uncertain.

The household has extraordinary command over housing, mobility, knowledge, care and time. Yachts, palaces and great portfolios would scarcely improve its Tuesday.

Outside the home, the megawatt economy carries civilisational loads. It desalinates seawater, closes material loops, grows medicines, restores landscapes, runs vast instruments and builds in orbit.

Particle accelerators, restored river systems and orbital foundries belong to civilisational agency. Beyond the household, surplus becomes knowledge, resilience, beauty and reach.

<DataInsight
  insight="Human consumption approaches enough. Human purpose keeps going."
/>

One life can still contain grief, loneliness, envy and failure. Abundance removes the preventable constraints that consume possibility before love and purpose get a chance.

One body is still one body. A day is still twenty-four hours.

We can make the means to live those hours safely, freely and well universal.

**Abundance is enough.**

### <span id="appendix-a" class="appendix-anchor"></span>A. Claim Register

*Abundance combines claims with different confidence levels.*

| Claim | Register | What the evidence earns now | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private consumption bends toward enough | Measured behaviour + design modelling | Wealth gains change current spending less among richer and more-liquid households. Researchers can model finite service bundles | Evidence leaves a universal happiness plateau and wealth threshold unestablished |
| Raw planetary energy flux exceeds one megawatt per person | Arithmetic on measured physical quantities | Earth's solar denominator exceeds the horizon | Collection, conversion, storage, land, materials and ecology |
| Energy, intelligence and automation compound | Emerging mechanism | Bounded factories already combine all three | Reliable robotics and cost diffusion across the heterogeneous economy |
| Biology becomes an engineering substrate | Demonstrated tools + emerging production system | Structure prediction, gene editing and industrial biomanufacturing are real | Delivery, validation, scale-up, clinical proof and broad control of ageing |
| Everyone can hold a durable claim on output | Institutional design | Prices, public infrastructure and shared capital are known channels | Political durability, supply response and capture |
| Humans retain authorship over ends | Normative constitutional claim | Competence and legitimate authority are distinct | The institutions of delegation, contestability and possible machine moral status |

Each register earns a limited inference. Solar flux measures the resource. Grid economics determines delivery. Gene editing proves a tool. Clinical systems determine abundant medicine. Productive machines expand output. Institutions determine who benefits.

### <span id="appendix-b" class="appendix-anchor"></span>B. The Three Boundaries of Enough

*Keynes found the demand boundary. Hirsch found the positional boundary. Sen found the access boundary. The new variable is the supply machine.*

Keynes separated absolute wants from relative wants in 1930. Veblen had already described consumption used to display rank. Hirsch later formalised the split between an expandable material economy and a positional economy limited by nature, congestion or exclusion.<sup><a href="#ref-26">[26]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-28">[28]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-29">[29]</a></sup>

Keynes imagined four- to eightfold productivity growth and a fifteen-hour week. British output came close. The workweek stayed much longer.<sup><a href="#ref-27">[27]</a></sup> Productivity left status, unequal claims on output, new goods and the social meaning of work intact.

Sen supplied the missing distributional distinction. Aggregate food can exist while a person lacks the entitlement to command it. Commodities can exist while a person lacks the capability to turn them into a valued life.<sup><a href="#ref-30">[30]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-31">[31]</a></sup>

The essay extends the production side. Civilisation can increasingly reproduce clean-power capacity, encoded machine cognition and machine labour together. The old demand question becomes newly live because the supply machine is approaching it.

### <span id="appendix-c" class="appendix-anchor"></span>C. The Fifty-Million-Dollar Life

*Evidence identifies a bend. The essay supplies a deliberately generous service envelope.*

| Evidence | Result | What it establishes | Remaining boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Federal Reserve wealth analysis | Top-income households consumed about **0.8 cents** of the next wealth dollar, while the other four quintiles consumed about **7.5 cents**<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> | A sharp difference in marginal spending response | Additional wealth may retain some benefit |
| Norwegian lottery records | Low-liquidity recipients of small prizes spent nearly all within a year. High-liquidity recipients of large prizes spent slightly less than half<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup> | Balance sheets and liquidity change consumption | A universal stopping point remains unknown |
| Thirty-three-country aspiration survey | In **86 per cent of countries**, a majority selected **US\$10 million or less** for an ideal life. **8–39 per cent** chose unlimited wealth<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup> | Most respondents chose a finite amount | The survey measures stated aspirations. Revealed consumption remains untested |
| Wellbeing adversarial collaboration | Wellbeing often continued rising roughly with log income at high incomes<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup> | Wellbeing can keep rising beyond material comfort | Each additional dollar produces a smaller proportional change |

The body's **US\$20–50 million** range comes from capitalising an intentionally lavish **US\$500,000-to-US\$1-million annual personal consumption stream**:

<Callout type="info">

**\$500,000 ÷ 3% ≈ \$16.7 million**

**\$1 million ÷ 2% = \$50 million**

</Callout>

The lower result rounds upward to leave room for tax, fees, volatility and unusually expensive years. The target includes excellent housing, care, mobility, education, leisure and time. It excludes the same harbour view, original artwork, political office, corporate control and open-ended project finance. Those belong to position or agency. Reproducible personal services form the target.

The relevant question asks whether production can make the services that fortune buys today ordinary.

### <span id="appendix-d" class="appendix-anchor"></span>D. The Physical Service Envelope

*A life is finite enough to model. The megawatt describes the supply system behind it.*

Millward-Hopkins and colleagues began with services. They specified shelter, nutrition, hygiene, mobility, healthcare, education and communication, then worked backward to the required materials and energy. Their efficient 2050 scenario used **15.3 GJ of final energy per person per year**.<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup>

A later Swiss study widened the inventory to include furnishings, cleaning products, public indoor space, security and research, then compared the model with Switzerland's full consumption footprint.<sup><a href="#ref-32">[32]</a></sup> One kilowatt of continuous average power equals about **31.6 GJ per year**:

| Service boundary | Final energy per person per year | Continuous average | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Global decent-living scenario, 2050 | 15.3 GJ | 0.49 kW | Bottom-up, highly efficient sufficiency model |
| Swiss decent-living scenario, 2050 | 19.5 GJ | 0.62 kW | Bottom-up model with a wider public and household inventory |
| Swiss net-zero scenario retaining current service demands, 2050 | ~85 GJ | ~2.7 kW | Modelled full consumption footprint implied by continuing current living standards |
| Swiss full consumption footprint, 2019 | 147 GJ | 4.7 kW | Accounted direct and embodied energy supporting households, government, non-profits and infrastructure |
| Age of Wonders supply horizon | 31,536 GJ | 1,000 kW | Total productive capacity divided by population |

The rows use different accounting boundaries. The decent-living cases are normative minimums. The Swiss footprint includes direct and embodied energy across households, government and infrastructure. The megawatt belongs to the entire productive civilisation.

The comparison reveals the scale. A rich society's current footprint sits in single-digit kilowatts per person. One megawatt is roughly **200 times** the accounted Swiss footprint and **2,000 times** the decent-living model. The remainder supplies industry, computation, restoration, infrastructure and exploration.

A human metabolism supplies roughly **97 watts** from a 2,000-kilocalorie daily intake. The horizon is about ten thousand human metabolisms of productive power per person.

### <span id="appendix-e" class="appendix-anchor"></span>E. The Megawatt Horizon

*The raw solar denominator permits the horizon. Engineering must deliver it.*

NASA's global energy budget gives average incoming solar radiation of roughly **340 W/m²** at the top of the atmosphere and **240 W/m²** absorbed after reflection.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup> Across Earth's 510 trillion square metres:

<Callout type="info">

**340 W/m² × 510 × 10¹² m² ≈ 173,000 TW incoming**

**240 W/m² × 510 × 10¹² m² ≈ 122,000 TW absorbed**

</Callout>

The body's horizon equals **8,000 TW** across eight billion people:

| Comparison | Ratio |
|---|---:|
| Current global primary energy: 19.7 TW | ~406× |
| Incoming planetary solar flux: 173,000 TW | 4.6% |
| Absorbed planetary solar flux: 122,000 TW | 6.6% |

The percentages compare productive power with raw radiant flux before collection, conversion and storage losses. They serve as physical denominators. A deployment plan must absorb every loss and constraint.

The boundary difference is decisive. The IPCC reports roughly **120,000 TW** of sunlight reaching the surface. It also cites a land-constrained PV technical potential near **1,080 EJ/year**, or about **34 TW average**.<sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup> Incident flux answers whether the energy exists. Technical potential asks what a specified technology, land envelope and siting regime could collect. Delivered firm power adds finance, grids, storage and reliability.

The Energy Institute records **620 EJ** of global primary energy in 2023, equivalent to **19.7 TW**.<sup><a href="#ref-33">[33]</a></sup> IRENA's 2026 cost register shows renewable generation remaining competitive across much new capacity while financing, grids and storage determine delivered cost.<sup><a href="#ref-10">[10]</a></sup>

An 8,000 TW civilisation therefore requires more than today's terrestrial build pattern: massive clean generation, storage, transmission, material recirculation and eventually collection or industry beyond the biosphere. The resource permits the direction. The engineering programme must earn it.

### <span id="appendix-f" class="appendix-anchor"></span>F. Firm Power and the Spillway

*Annual matching takes division. Firm supply takes system design.*

A one-gigawatt continuous load consumes **8.76 TWh each year**. At a 25 per cent solar capacity factor, matching that annual energy begins with:

<Callout type="info">

**1 GW ÷ 25% = 4 GW of solar nameplate**

</Callout>

Four gigawatts matches annual energy and leaves the night unsupplied. Firmness depends on the weather record, target availability, storage duration, overbuild, transmission, complementary generation and backup. IRENA's 2026 portfolios show high-renewable firm supply becoming competitive at favourable sites. A modelled **95 per cent** reliability case falls far short of five-nines digital availability.<sup><a href="#ref-9">[9]</a></sup>

Datacentres make the architecture concrete because they combine large loads, high interruption costs and some movable work. The IEA counted **415 TWh** of datacentre electricity use in 2024 and projects roughly **945 TWh** in its 2030 base case.<sup><a href="#ref-11">[11]</a></sup> Lawrence Berkeley identifies four flexibility layers: compute scheduling, facility management, storage and onsite generation.<sup><a href="#ref-12">[12]</a></sup>

An overbuilt campus dispatches each unit of output toward the highest-value use available:

| Order | Destination | Boundary |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Critical compute and safety systems | Must remain firm |
| 2 | Flexible compute | Only some work moves in time or place |
| 3 | Batteries and grid export | Losses, duration, interconnection and market access |
| 4 | Heat, water, hydrogen and synthetic fuels | Lower conversion efficiency. Dedicated equipment needs utilisation |
| 5 | Curtailment | The final unit is worth less than the equipment needed to capture it |

Google has demonstrated non-urgent compute shifting across hours or regions and clean generation feeding local grids where contracts allow.<sup><a href="#ref-13">[13]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-14">[14]</a></sup> NREL shows why deliberate overbuild and some curtailment can lower whole-system cost.<sup><a href="#ref-15">[15]</a></sup>

The spillway is conditional. Surplus exists at a node, in an hour, under a contract. Storage and flexible demand progressively bid it away. Time, place and market access determine its value.

### <span id="appendix-g" class="appendix-anchor"></span>G. The Conversion Stack

*Power becomes abundance only after every layer between an electron and a life closes.*

<Callout type="info">

**Manufactured clean energy**

**× reproducible machine intelligence**

**× physical conversion infrastructure**

**× institutional permission and trust**

**= delivered goods and services**

</Callout>

The home in the body exposes the gearbox:

| Layer | What it does | Remaining constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Clean power | Runs quarry, kiln, furnace, factory and site | Firm supply, grids and delivered cost |
| Intelligence | Designs for the site, schedules work, finds faults | Wrong objectives, poor data and verification |
| Automation | Repeats modules, moves mass, fastens and inspects | Reliability outside controlled environments |
| Materials | Converts cement, glass, timber and steel into shelter | Extraction, recycling, logistics and ecological limits |
| Local permission | Assigns land, planning approval and connection | Scarcity, delay, monopoly and political refusal |
| Access | Lets a household command the finished service | Price, ownership, public provision and distribution |

The first four layers can push repeatable work onto manufactured-cost curves. The site stays scarce. A megawatt economy can make an excellent building abundant while Sydney Harbour remains singular.

The recursive loop is real in bounded settings. Electricity powers machines. Machines build energy equipment. Software raises yield. Cumulative production lowers unit cost. Its civilisation-scale strength remains emerging. Critical materials, maintenance, cyber risk, weak competition, capital misallocation and ecological damage can interrupt the loop.

Energy and intelligence are unusually general inputs. Materials, land, capital and institutions complete the production function.

### <span id="appendix-h" class="appendix-anchor"></span>H. Prediction Is Not Treatment

*The biological programme is a chain of gates. Each advance faces another test.*

Industrial biology manufactures enzymes, ingredients, materials and therapeutics.<sup><a href="#ref-18">[18]</a></sup> AlphaFold 3 expands the range of biomolecular complexes it can predict.<sup><a href="#ref-16">[16]</a></sup> Casgevy proved that clinicians can edit a patient's own blood-forming stem cells outside the body, return them and use them in an approved treatment.<sup><a href="#ref-17">[17]</a></sup>

The remaining chain is longer than the word *discovery* suggests:

| Gate | Question that must close | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement | What changed in this patient or cell? | Incomplete or noisy biology |
| Inference | Which mechanism or target matters? | Causation remains unresolved |
| Experiment | Does the intervention work in a model system? | Prediction fails validation |
| Delivery | Can it reach the right cells safely? | Off-target effects or immune response |
| Manufacture | Can it be made repeatedly at clinical quality? | Titre, rate, yield, purification or capital cost |
| Clinical proof | Does benefit exceed harm in people? | Efficacy, safety or trial failure |
| Access | Can the health system deliver it broadly? | Specialist capacity, logistics and price |

Industrial reviews identify scale-up as the persistent economic gate beyond molecular imagination.<sup><a href="#ref-19">[19]</a></sup> Casgevy itself requires collection, conditioning, cell processing and reinfusion. A programmable therapy can still be a scarce medical procedure.

The hallmarks-of-ageing framework organises interacting mechanisms and candidate interventions while leaving control of ageing unproven.<sup><a href="#ref-20">[20]</a></sup> The US National Institute on Aging continues to warn that marketed anti-ageing interventions lack solid evidence.<sup><a href="#ref-21">[21]</a></sup>

The essay declares “a century of health” as an engineering horizon: prevent predictable failures, correct inherited defects, destroy mutations, retrain hostile immune responses and replace failing tissue. The tools establish direction. Clinical evidence must establish arrival.

### <span id="appendix-i" class="appendix-anchor"></span>I. A Durable Claim on Output

*Real supply must answer every nominal claim.*

The essay's “share of the machines” means a durable claim on machine-generated output. A brokerage account offers one form among many. Three channels can carry that claim:

| Channel | Mechanism | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Falling prices | Productivity makes reproducible services cheaper | Competition must pass cost declines through and prevent capture as rent |
| Universal infrastructure | Power, care, education, mobility and connectivity become dependable public or regulated systems | Capacity, quality, queues and fiscal durability |
| Productive ownership | Pensions, employee equity, cooperatives, citizen funds and dividends share capital returns | Governance, concentration, volatility and capture |

A constrained market turns new cash into higher bids for the same homes and clinicians. The World Bank's UBI review makes supply response and market integration central to price effects. The OECD shows the fiscal trade-offs when flat income replaces or supplements targeted systems.<sup><a href="#ref-24">[24]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-25">[25]</a></sup>

A durable abundance floor therefore has five tests:

1. Claims rise only with realised productive capacity.
2. Public services and physical supply expand with purchasing power.
3. Shared capital preserves its principal across generations.
4. Governance is transparent, plural and resistant to capture.
5. Markets, private enterprise and greater wealth can continue above the floor.

The Alaska Permanent Fund proves that shared intergenerational capital can distribute returns to citizens.<sup><a href="#ref-22">[22]</a></sup> Its evidence stops short of stable, politically shareable automation rents. Finland's basic-income experiment found little employment change and improved perceived security and wellbeing among a defined unemployed group.<sup><a href="#ref-23">[23]</a></sup> The experiment covered a defined unemployed group and left economy-wide supply untested.

The claim is constitutional. Every person needs reliable command over enough real output. The instrument can vary. Selling hours can carry only part of that entitlement once machines perform more of the work.

### <span id="appendix-j" class="appendix-anchor"></span>J. Authorship After Automation

*The essay grounds human standing in personhood.*

The capability claim is deliberately strong. Machines may eventually design homes, cities and institutions better than humans. Human standing survives that outcome.

Political legitimacy concerns the right to exercise power. Superior prediction supplies competence alone.<sup><a href="#ref-34">[34]</a></sup>

Institutions can delegate authority to automated systems when people authorise the aims, audit the procedures, contest the decisions and revise the mandate.<sup><a href="#ref-35">[35]</a></sup> Human authorship means standing in that process. Institutions can delegate execution.

Human values remain plural, incomplete, changing and sometimes irreconcilable. Alignment therefore requires social choice among stakeholders.<sup><a href="#ref-36">[36]</a></sup>

Current science leaves machine consciousness unresolved.<sup><a href="#ref-37">[37]</a></sup> If machines acquire morally relevant experience, the constituency expands beyond humanity.

### <span id="appendix-k" class="appendix-anchor"></span>K. Falsifier Register

*A horizon earns confidence by naming the observations that would shrink it.*

Recorded August 2026. Some claims can fail by recomputation, some only through deployment, and one through constitutional practice.

**K.1: The demand bend, checkable now.** <a class="xref" href="#appendix-c">Appendix C</a> claims a bend. If better household data show marginal private material consumption remaining proportional to wealth across the extreme upper tail, the fifty-million-dollar service envelope weakens as a social model. The physical claim that one body consumes finite services survives. The economic claim that ordinary affluence bends toward enough fails.

**K.2: The megawatt arithmetic, checkable today.** <a class="xref" href="#appendix-d">Appendix D</a> and <a class="xref" href="#appendix-e">E</a> expose every denominator: population, current primary energy, service footprints and solar flux. A reader can recompute them now. A hard ecological or material plateau far below the horizon would force the megawatt economy off Earth, shrink it or invalidate it.

**K.3: The conversion gate, visible in deployment.** A home still fails to appear when robots remain unreliable outside controlled factories, grids block new loads or landowners capture falling production costs as rent. The indicators are task coverage, total cost of ownership, build time and delivered service prices. If they plateau while compute and generation continue improving, the abundance stack in <a class="xref" href="#appendix-g">Appendix G</a> has a missing gearbox.

**K.4: The biological gate, visible at scale-up.** The programme depends on candidates surviving validation, delivery, manufacture and clinical proof. Better molecular prediction opens the first gate. Watch commercial titre, rate, yield, purification cost, trial success and treatment throughput. If biomanufacturing remains confined to high-value, low-volume products and ageing interventions repeatedly fail in humans, “a century of health” retreats from programme to metaphor.

**K.5: The access gate, visible in ordinary lives.** Output can rise while households become less secure. Track median real service consumption, housing burden, healthcare access, waiting time, wealth concentration and monopoly rent. If productivity growth continues to capitalise mainly into land and ownership while the material floor stagnates, the essay has described a private empire of machines.

**K.6: The ecological gate, visible at every doubling.** Efficiency can increase total throughput through rebound.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup> Track absolute extraction, land conversion, emissions, biodiversity loss and waste as clean capacity grows. Rising damage would break the ecological boundary. More energy would enlarge agency while consuming the world it was meant to enrich.

**K.7: The constitutional authorship gate.** A system fails this test when people lose the ability to discover its aims, contest its decisions or revise the institutions that delegate power to it. Accuracy leaves that constitutional failure intact. If automation makes civilisation less answerable to the persons affected by it, machine competence has displaced human authorship.
