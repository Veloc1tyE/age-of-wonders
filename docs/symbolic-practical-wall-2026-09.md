The new formal example in F states a conditional information-loss result: if the complete surviving code is identical for two observations whose correct later answers differ, the code alone cannot supply both correct answers. Additional evidence can change that result. Expanding a declared family of questions restricts adequate compressions, but a compact sufficient model may still cover the expanded family. No growth exponent follows.

The elementary Lyapunov model can require only linearly more precision bits as horizon increases. The existing exponential work-rate example additionally assumes a specified numerical method's refinement, error and cost laws. It remains illustrative; it is not a lower bound over digital investigators.

Humans face information loss too. New mapmaking may reorganise already retained evidence. Digital investigators can use physical experiments directly, revise learning rules, copy acquired state, and distribute improvements. The research-enclave rival remains eligible to overturn the biological division even when its persistent investigators are expensive.
