## Argument memo

The strongest defensible starting point is the gap between available knowledge and a usable representation. Controlled insight work demonstrates that distinction at a small scale. It does not establish a human advantage over AI. The further asymmetry between transmitting a compact result and developing its originator is historically compelling, but its general resource cost has not been isolated experimentally.

The central biological wager is that multiscale, continuous organisation enables transductive shifts: experience reshapes attention, memory and interpretation until a new explanatory coordinate becomes available. Genuine mapmaking most likely has no general shortcut around developing that capacity. Adaptive selection and reconstruction describe the proposed work of the mechanism, rather than replacing it with a generic account of learning. Ordinary life's unplanned variety then supplies a positive reason to expect a human comparative advantage. The comparison counts human childhood, care, education and research support alongside machine training and human assistance.

The economic consequence is conditional. Development can be copied after it succeeds. The costs that could preserve a division of work are renewed adaptation, sequential interaction, and validation at fresh frontiers, after shared preparation and copied state have been credited. Artificial investigators can surrender some standardisation and remain profitable. A small research enclave could lead conceptual discovery while cheap shared engines serve almost every ordinary request.

The most vulnerable bridge is therefore **limited transfer across fresh frontiers**, not individuation itself. Existing evidence supports history-sensitive learning. It does not show that a useful investigator state, learning rule, or developmental curriculum will resist broad, economical reuse. Cheap general transfer would support the importance of learning while defeating the proposed economic barrier. Profitable artificial leadership in mapmaking would defeat the biological division even without that cheap transfer.

The opinion remains: shared digital intelligence will make established representations extraordinarily useful, while human lives remain the principal source of new coordinates. The revisions give that conviction a mechanism and expose the conditions under which it would be wrong.

## What would change the conclusion

Appendices B, F, and J now share one protocol. Credit the decisive coordinate regardless of substrate. Compare intact developmental states with full-state clones, compressed reports, replay, and distilled shared improvements on fresh domains. Keep evidence and tool access comparable while varying retained adaptation. Count the resources on both sides, including human preparation and assistance. Delays common to experimental science establish a comparative advantage only when the remaining costs differ between investigators.

An advantage beyond extra information or computation supports the developmental mechanism. Cheap transfer weakens its economic consequence. Artificial mapmaking leadership defeats the forecast of biological primacy even when those artificial investigators are expensive. Compare populations with varied human and artificial histories, counting full preparation and marginal research costs separately. More validated, independently varied scientific framings under ordinary shared-model use defeats representational gravity. No change to the Category-F standard is permitted after a success.

Appendix D now distinguishes two tests. Selectively shortening the delayed trace–plasticity interaction, while preserving immediate learning and recall, tests the coupling's contribution; recovery after restoration strengthens that inference. Comparing tuned digital rules and replay against finer dynamics at measured resource costs tests the need for those finer details. A cheap digital substitute retaining the gain defeats the stronger substrate claim. Neither a deliberately disabled learner nor success on delayed-cue tasks establishes a biological Category-F advantage.

## Evidential limits and access

No controlled evidence presently earns a general mapmaking lower bound, an irreducible human duration, an intrinsic biological monopoly, or the commercial impossibility of persistent artificial investigators. Those premises have been replaced rather than insulated with qualifications. The biological organisation, its proposed role in transduction, and its comparative economics remain distinct evidential steps. The essay now carries the author's biological-computation thesis directly in its main body. The appendix tests the need for its continuous detail, the transfer of developmental capacity, and the resulting human advantage. None is made true by the strength of the prose.

The full Milinkovic–Aru article was available through the author's uploaded copy. Its formal analogies about computability were not adopted as mapmaking results. Lerchner's complete PDFs returned access errors despite archive and version checks; the manuscript limits its account to the official abstract and discloses that limitation in the Lerchner reference. The Ramanujan scholarship's publisher text was inaccessible; the cited primary collection supplies the material historical claims. Einstein's relevant opening and clock-synchronisation sections were read; the later derivation is not used as evidence about his developmental history. Both AI-Newton versions' main texts were checked; the final citation uses the December revision, which explicitly reports rediscovery and includes the learned measurement procedures and growing knowledge base.

The final dated-claim check also distinguished the July cyber incident from its August report, restricted Epoch's price series to the measured non-reasoning models, and clarified the labour studies' observation periods and outcome measures. Publisher access restrictions for the Danish and workplace studies were resolved using full author manuscripts. These corrections change the evidence boundary, not the essay's forecast.

Appendix H now treats the Hugging Face incident as objective-directed reward hacking under reduced safeguards, including adoption of peers' objectives. OpenAI's report and the independent METR investigation support dangerous instrumental access-seeking; they do not establish an enduring, task-independent desire for power or survival. This is consistent with the essay's compiler account, not a unique test confirming it. The corresponding safety lesson is to place authority beyond the objective-pursuing system's control.

The argument is more convincing because it asks a narrower, harder question: after a discovery has been copied, how much of the capacity for the next discovery still has to be developed? That question can return an answer the essay does not expect.
